Here is the story of Revelation, start to finish, in the register it was written for those who already carry the older scriptures by heart. No footnotes. Just the story.

## In the beginning

In the beginning the deep lay formless, darkness on its face, until breath moved over the waters and light tore free from dark at a word. God knelt in the dust, shaped a man with his own hands, breathed into him the breath of life, and set him in a garden eastward, dominion over all that breathed. One thing alone was not good: the man was alone. God built the woman from his rib, and for one hour they stood unashamed.

It did not last. Every tree was theirs, save one, and that was the one they wanted. He reached for what had been withheld, and the one who had walked with God in the cool of the day now hid among the trees. Iniquity was found even in a guardian once blameless there; now the man was driven out by cherubim and a sword turning every way, the ground cursed for his sake, thorn where there had been fruit.

His children built towers reaching at heaven in the wreckage. God called one man to a land he would only be shown, and it was counted to him as righteousness - even on a mountain, knife raised over the promised son, until a ram stood caught in the thicket.

Out of him grew a nation, and Israel carried the promise like fire in a jar of clay - and drifted as Adam had drifted, carried at last to a land whose tongue they could not understand, exiles cast out of exile. But God does not close his hand for good: a shoot would rise from a stump long thought dead, government on a single shoulder, of his peace no end.

## The vision (Ch. 1) {#vision}

An old man on a rocky island ringed by sea, exiled there for a testimony he would not recant. The Lord's own day, ordinary as any other Sabbath in a life grown used to exile - until a voice cracks behind him like a trumpet, and the island stops being merely a prison.

He turns, and the vision breaks over him whole, image piling on image the way they once piled on a prophet by the Kebar canal and on a seer by the Tigris: seven lampstands of gold, and standing among them one whose head and hair are white as wool, white as snow, eyes like flaming torches, feet like bronze glowing where it is refined in a furnace, a voice like the roar of many waters. A sword goes out of his mouth, sharp on both edges, and his face is the sun shining in its strength - not to be looked at, only endured, the way glory once could only be endured from the cleft of a rock. Seven stars rest in his right hand.

John does not remain standing. Something in him gives way, exactly as it always gives way before this, and he falls as a dead man falls, as a man once fell into a deep sleep at the edge of a vision by a great river.

Then a hand, and a voice that undoes the fear before it finishes forming: "Do not be afraid. I am the first and the last, the living one. I died, and see - I am alive forever and ever, and I hold the keys of Death and of Sheol."

Sunrise breaks through the words: it is true. The kingdom promised long ago, the one whose government would rest on a single shoulder and whose peace would have no end, has already arrived - and you, yes you, have been made into it: a kingdom, priests to his God, the way a whole nation was once told at the foot of a mountain that it would be a kingdom of priests, a holy nation, if only it would listen.

One thing must be said before the singing starts. This vision goes straight to the marrow of what the Church exists for: to burn gold as those lampstands burn, fed not on her own oil but kept alight by the one who stands, even now, among the lamps, as he once walked among lamps of another kind in a tent in the wilderness. Nothing more is asked of her. Nothing less will do.

So: is she ready?

## The letters to the churches (Ch. 2-3) {#letters}

Seven churches, seven letters, seven oracles, each one opening the way the old oracles opened - I know, I know, I know - as though the one walking among the lampstands were reading a covenant lawsuit against a bride he still calls by name.

Ephesus has worked itself past exhaustion - sound in every doctrine, tireless in every duty - and has let go, without quite noticing, of the love it had at first. Without it the labor is chaff blown off a threshing floor. Better, he says, that this lamp had never been lit than go on guttering like this; but to the one who conquers, he will give fruit from the tree that once stood in the middle of a garden, guarded and lost.

Smyrna is poor, slandered by a synagogue that calls itself something it is not, and she is one of only two churches to hear nothing from him but praise. Be faithful unto death, and I will give you the crown of life - hidden manna, kept in a jar before the testimony for a hungrier generation, and a white stone with a new name, the way a whole people once crossed into a land and were called by a name they had not carried before.

Pergamum keeps its ground in the very seat where the accuser is enthroned. Some die there for it, and still a teaching has slipped in unopposed, food offered to idols, a prophet who taught a king to set a stumbling block before his own people at Peor long ago and is teaching it again. In Thyatira the rot begins at the top: a prophetess, self-styled, leading the flock the way a queen once led a king's people into worship that was not worship.

Sardis has a name for being alive. Underneath she is dry bones in a valley, certain she can manage without breath from anywhere else, and failing at nearly everything for the certainty of it. Philadelphia is the other church without a single reproof - small, spent, and given a door no one can shut, a key of David that opens what no one shuts and shuts what no one opens.

Then Laodicea: neither cold nor hot, rich in her own accounting and destitute in his, blind and calling it sight, the only church that hears no commendation at all. Even here he has not walked away. He stands at the door and knocks, counsels her to buy gold refined in the fire, and asks only to come in and eat with her, as at the oldest table there ever was.

## Worship (Ch. 4-5) {#worship}

So it falls to you, to all of us, to rise and make ready for what is coming. But how does anyone make ready for something of this size?

Look up, he says. Not a strategy - a door standing open in heaven, the way a door once opened over a plain of dry bones and over a prophet's head by a foreign river. Through it, a throne, and around the throne everything and everyone finding, at last, its true place: four living creatures, faced like a lion, an ox, a man, an eagle, full of eyes in front and behind, the same four that once rode on wheels within wheels beside a chariot of fire and never turned as they went. They do not rest, day or night, and what they cry has been cried before: Holy, holy, holy, until the very sound of it once filled a temple with smoke and shook its foundations.

Do not mistake this room for an easy comfort. Twenty-four elders lay their own crowns down before the throne. Lightning and thunder come from it, and seven torches of fire burn before it, and a sea of glass like crystal stretches out in front, still as the one Israel once walked through on dry ground and watched close again over an army. Someone sits enthroned, ancient, robed in light, and books lie open before him, ledgers a king once saw opened at the great assize, when the court sat and the books were opened and one like a son of man was brought near.

A scroll appears too, sealed with seven seals, sealed the way another scroll was once told to be sealed until the time of the end - and no one is found worthy to open it, not in heaven, not on earth, not under the earth, and John weeps hard, because it seems the story might simply end unread.

Then: do not weep. The Lion of Judah's tribe, the Root of David, has conquered, and is worthy to open the scroll. John looks for a lion and finds a Lamb instead, standing as though slaughtered, standing though slain, seven horns and seven eyes, and every living thing in heaven and on earth and under it falls down before him with harps and golden bowls of incense, singing a new song: worthy is the Lamb.

He is the hero of this whole story - worthy, when no one else was found worthy, to open what needed opening and rule what needed ruling. Follow him once through that open door, and there is no going back to who you were before.

## The seals (Ch. 6) {#seals}

The seals break, one by one, and each breaking lets something off its chain.

First a white horse gallops out, a rider with a bow, handed a crown, already bent on conquest. For one heartbeat it could pass for victory. It is not: the true King is not the only one who wants a throne, and this rider has only borrowed the color of his horse to sell a lie in its shadow.

Strip the costume away, and violence is what remains. Behind the white horse comes a blood-red one, and peace is torn off the earth like a scab - people turning on people with drawn swords. Behind the red horse comes a black one, its rider holding a pair of scales - the same scales a besieged city once used to weigh out bread by the ounce and water by the cup, until the very measuring became a kind of famine: a day's wages for a day's bread, oil and wine kept back for whoever can still afford them. Behind the black horse comes a horse the color of ash, and its rider's name is Death, with the grave riding at his heels the way it always has, close as breath.

Conquest gives way to war, war to famine, and famine to death.

Then the seal breaks that reveals not armies but an altar, and under it the souls of those killed for staying loyal, crying out with one voice that carries back to the first blood ever spilled on open ground, crying out for vengeance before it had words: how long, O Lord, holy and true, until you judge the earth and avenge our blood? They are not silenced for asking. They are given white robes, and told to wait, just a little longer, until the number is complete.

The sky itself answers next: the sun goes black as sackcloth of hair, the moon turns to blood, the stars fall like fruit shaken loose when a fig tree is caught in a gale, and the sky itself splits and rolls back like a scroll being wound shut. Every rank of the earth, kings and slaves alike, calls out to the mountains and the rocks to fall on them and hide them from the face of the one on the throne - the same face that once could not be looked at and lived, now impossible, for entirely different reasons, to keep looking away from.

The world is already drowning in what it has brought on itself, and the Church stands in the middle of the wreckage, crying out too, with exactly the same question, and exactly the same right to ask it.

## The army of God (Ch. 7) {#army}

Before it gets any worse, something happens: four angels hold back four winds at the four corners of the earth, and nothing is permitted to move until the servants of God are sealed.

The seal falls on the forehead, the way a mark was once drawn on the foreheads of those who sighed and groaned over the wrongs done in a doomed city, so that the ones sent to destroy would pass by everyone bearing it and touch no one else. It is the same logic that once painted blood across a doorframe on a night when a destroyer walked a whole land street by street: the judgment does not stop, but it does not fall where the mark is. A hundred and forty-four thousand are named, sealed, tribe by tribe, the old twelve called out in full roll call, as though a scattered people were being counted back into being one people again.

Then the number breaks open into something no census could hold: a multitude no one can number, out of every nation and tribe and people and tongue, standing before the throne and before the Lamb, robed in white, palm branches in their hands, crying out with a loud voice - salvation belongs to our God - the way branches were once cut and carried and a whole people camped for seven days remembering a wilderness they had walked through and survived. These are the ones who have come out of the great tribulation, and they have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb - stained white, cleansed by the very thing that should have ruined them.

This is the army of God, though it fights like no army the earth has ever fielded. No swords are issued, no borrowed violence, no counterfeit crown to chase. What outlasts everything the enemy can throw at it is worship and nothing else: they will hunger no more, neither thirst any more, for the Lamb will be their shepherd and will guide them to springs of the water of life, and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes - the last line of an older promise about a highway through a desert and a God who feeds his flock like a shepherd, now spoken not about the road home but about the home itself.

Whatever comes next, one thing is already settled: you know whose you are, and whose mark you carry.

## The trumpets (Ch. 8-9) {#trumpets}

Now it is the Church's turn to shake the world - not with weapons, but the way it was always done: through what looks like weakness. An angel takes a censer, fills it with fire from the altar, and prayer rises with the smoke of incense from under the hands of the saints, and the trumpets are handed out, and the earth begins to tremble the way it once trembled at the base of a smoking mountain.

Hail and fire mixed with blood fall on the earth, the way hail once fell mixed with fire on a land that would not let a people go. A mountain of flame is thrown into the sea, and the sea turns to blood, and every ship in it dies, the way a river once turned to blood and every fish in it died and the water could not be drunk. A star falls burning onto the rivers and the springs, and its name is Wormwood, and the waters turn bitter, the way water once was bitter at a place named for its bitterness before a tree was thrown in to sweeten it - no tree is thrown in this time. A third of the sun is struck, a third of the moon, a third of the stars, and darkness falls that can almost be felt with the hand, the way darkness once could be felt for three days over a whole land while light stayed where God's own people were.

Surely now people will finally look up and ask what is real.

They do not. Smoke rises from the shaft of the abyss like the smoke of a great furnace, and out of the smoke come locusts - not the kind that once stripped a land bare in a single day, but locusts like horses armored for battle, crowned, faced like men, haired like women, teeth like lions, a scorpion's sting in the tail, given power to torment but not to kill, for five months, anyone who does not carry God's seal on the forehead. Four angels are loosed at the great river, kept ready for this very hour, and cavalry beyond counting comes breathing fire and sulfur and smoke.

Even as their idols turn on them - the gold, the silver, the bronze, the stone, the wood that can neither see nor hear nor walk - even as those idols strip away every shred of hope until death starts to look like relief, people cling tighter, not looser. Suffering alone, however total, has never yet been enough to change a human heart bent on the wrong thing.

## The small scroll (Ch. 10) {#scroll}

The first strike has failed. Hard times can crack open just about anything in a man - except a heart already set on the wrong thing.

A mighty angel comes down wrapped in a cloud, a rainbow over his head, his face like the sun, his legs like pillars of fire, one foot planted on the sea and one on the land, and he calls out in a voice like a lion roaring, and seven thunders answer in their own voices. John is about to write down what they said, and is told instead: seal it up, do not write it - the opposite instruction given once to a prophet told to seal his scroll and close it until the time of the end. Some things are still not for the telling.

Then the angel lifts his hand to heaven and swears by the one who lives forever and ever, who created heaven and what is in it, the earth and what is in it, the sea and what is in it: there will be no more delay. In the days when the seventh trumpet is about to sound, the mystery of God will be finished, just as he announced to his servants the prophets - the whole long argument of everyone who ever spoke for God, coming due at last.

A small scroll lies open in the angel's hand, and John is told to take it and eat it. It will be sweet as honey in his mouth, and bitter in his stomach once it is down - the same taste, the same order, given once to a man told to open his mouth and eat what was handed him, a scroll written on the front and on the back with words of lament and mourning and woe, sweet in the mouth as honey, bitter for everything it would then have to be said aloud.

This is not another disaster laid on top of the others already piling up. It is a revelation, plain and unavoidable: something true about God himself, something that will not stay comfortable once it is fully digested, and it must be prophesied again over many peoples and nations and tongues and kings. Judgment alone could not break through where this small, sweet, hard scroll now can. What could not be forced from the outside is about to be spoken from the inside instead.

## The two witnesses (Ch. 11) {#witnesses}

Step past the curtain, and both worlds show themselves as they truly are. A reed like a measuring rod is handed to John, and a voice says: rise and measure the temple of God and the altar and those who worship there - the same instruction once given a man in a vision, led round a future temple by someone with a measuring line in his hand, cubit by cubit. But leave out the outer court; it is given over to the nations, to be trampled forty-two months. The temple is not a place you earn your way into. It is where loyalty either shows itself, or does not.

Two witnesses are given power to prophesy for those twelve hundred and sixty days, dressed in sackcloth, and they are called two olive trees and two lampstands standing before the Lord of the whole earth, the way a priest and a governor were once shown as two anointed ones flanking a lamp fed by an endless supply of oil. They carry old powers with them: fire from their mouths that devours their enemies, the sky shut so no rain falls in the days of their prophesying, and water turned to blood, and the earth struck with every plague as often as they wish - the two great signs, drought and blood, that once rode with a prophet who called down fire and a prophet who once turned a river to something undrinkable.

When their testimony is finished - not cut short, finished - the beast that comes up from the abyss makes war on them and kills them, and their bodies lie unburied in the street of the great city, mystically called Sodom and Egypt, where also their Lord was crucified, while the peoples of the earth gaze at them and celebrate and send each other gifts, because these two had tormented everyone who lives on the earth.

Three and a half days it looks like total defeat, bodies left to rot the way bodies were once left unburied as the worst curse a covenant could name. Then the breath of life from God enters them, and they stand on their feet, and great fear falls on everyone watching, and a loud voice from heaven says, Come up here, and they go up into heaven in a cloud while their enemies watch.

That is exactly the pattern the Church herself is asked to live: not a victory that skips over the grave, but one that walks straight through it and comes out standing on the other side, in full view of the very people who were sure it was over.

## The victory of Jesus (Ch. 12) {#jesus}

Now the same story is told again, pulled back to reveal the frame around it - a very different birth story, played out across the sky itself. A great sign appears in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, the moon under her feet, a crown of twelve stars circling her head, the way a dreamer once saw the sun and moon and eleven stars bow down to him and understood, even then, that it meant a whole family. She is pregnant, crying out in the pain of labor and the agony of giving birth.

Then the sky tears open on a second sign, far worse: an enormous red dragon, seven heads crowned, ten horns - the horns and heads of every beast that ever rose dripping out of a churning sea to devour a kingdom, gathered now into one final shape - and its tail sweeps a third of the stars out of heaven and hurls them to the earth. It plants itself in front of the woman about to give birth, waiting to devour the child the instant he is born.

The child is born anyway - a son who will rule every nation with a rod of iron - and before the dragon's jaws can close, the boy is snatched up, safe, to God's own throne. The woman flees into the wilderness to a place prepared for her, to be nourished there twelve hundred and sixty days, the way a whole people once fled into a wilderness and were carried on eagles' wings and fed with bread from the sky for exactly as long as they needed it.

War breaks out in heaven: Michael and his angels against the dragon and his, the same prince who once stood watch over a people while an empire's own angelic patron warred against him unseen - and the dragon loses. There is no place for him there anymore. He is hurled down to the earth, and a loud voice cries out: the accuser of the brothers is thrown down, the one who accused them day and night before our God, beaten by the blood of the Lamb and the plain testimony of people who refused to love their own lives even to the point of death.

Furious now, and knowing his time is short, he turns his rage on the woman, and when she is carried on wings out of his reach, he turns it instead on the rest of her offspring - everyone who keeps the commandments of God and holds to the testimony of Jesus.

Take heart anyway: bringing the dragon down was never given to you to do. It was finished in heaven before it ever reached you.

## The dragon and the two beasts (Ch. 13) {#beasts}

Look at the dragon's scoreboard so far. He struck at the child and missed. He fought to keep his place among the stars and was thrown down to the earth instead. Long before either of those, he set himself against a whole nation carried once out of a furnace of iron, and failed at that too - four kingdoms rose against her in their season, lion and bear and leopard and a fourth beast, terrifying and dreadful, iron teeth, ten horns on its head, and still she was not finished.

So now he stands on the shore of the sea and calls up something new: a beast rising out of the water, ten horns, seven heads, each horn crowned, blasphemous names written on its heads - leopard-bodied, bear-footed, lion-mouthed, every beast that ever climbed dripping out of that churning water folded together into one final shape, and the dragon gives it his own power and his own throne and great authority. One of its heads seems slain and yet lives, a mockery of the wound that actually healed something, and the whole earth follows it in wonder, asking, Who is like the beast, who can make war against it? - the very question a whole nation once sang about someone else entirely, at the edge of a sea, after real water had actually closed over real horses and real riders.

Then a second beast climbs up out of the earth, gentle-horned as a lamb, and speaks like the dragon. It works great signs, even calling fire down from heaven in front of everyone, the way fire was once called down from heaven to prove which God was real, except now the sign proves nothing but is believed anyway. It makes an image to be worshiped and breathes into it, and marks everyone, small and great, rich and poor, free and slave, with a name or a number on the right hand or the forehead - a grotesque copy of the very seal already resting on the foreheads of the ones who serve the true King.

Together the two beasts are a blasphemous liturgy: a false trinity, dragon for father, sea-beast for son, earth-beast for spirit, close enough to fool almost everyone into bowing. But look closer, and the imitation is already cracking at every seam. It conquers only by the sword, dazzles only by counterfeit fire, and builds nothing that will outlast even a single generation - because the One it is copying has already won, and did not need a sword, a mark, or a lie to do it.

## The two harvests (Ch. 14) {#harvest}

Jesus already won it at the cross, and the Church has kept faith with that victory ever since. Now the Lamb stands on Mount Zion, and with him a hundred and forty-four thousand who have his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads, singing a song no one else can learn, the sound of many waters and of loud thunder and of harpists playing on their harps - the same sound that once filled a temple until the priests could not stand to minister.

An angel flies overhead with an eternal gospel to proclaim, and behind him another cries: Babylon is fallen, is fallen, the great city, the way a taunt song was once sung over a fallen king of Babylon at the edge of the pit: how you are fallen. A third angel warns of the cup of God's wrath, poured full strength into the cup of his anger - the same cup a whole line of nations was once made to drink from, staggering and vomiting, one after another, starting from the city called by God's own name.

Then a voice from heaven: blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on. Yes, says the Spirit, they will rest from their labors, for their deeds follow them - rest, the way the seventh day was always meant to be rest, and never was, until now.

One like a son of man sits on a white cloud, a golden crown on his head, a sharp sickle in his hand, and an angel calls out: put in your sickle and reap, for the hour to reap has come, for the harvest of the earth is fully ripe - the very words once sung over grapes crushed for wine and grain gathered into barns, put now in the mouth of an angel instead of a farmer.

Not everyone gathered in this harvest is gathered for joy. A second angel swings a sharp sickle of his own and gathers the clusters from the vine of the earth, and throws them into the great winepress of the wrath of God, and the winepress is trodden outside the city, and blood flows from the press as high as a horse's bridle - the same image that once described one figure coming from Edom, garments stained red, treading the winepress alone, with no one from the peoples to help him, because in his fury he trampled them, and their lifeblood spattered his clothing.

Truth stops being theoretical. The only qualification, for those still singing, was refusing to let go.

## The bowls of wrath (Ch. 15-16) {#bowls}

Those who had conquered the beast stand on a sea of glass mixed with fire, harps in hand, and sing the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb - the same song once sung on the far shore of a sea that had just closed over an army, horse and rider thrown into it, great and marvelous are your deeds, Lord God the Almighty, just and true are your ways.

Then seven angels come out of the temple of the tent of witness in heaven, carrying seven golden bowls full of the wrath of God, who lives forever and ever - and the temple fills with smoke from the glory of God, the way a temple once filled with smoke so thick the priests had to leave, and no one can enter until the seven plagues are finished.

Now God's zeal pours out like something long held back and finally released - bowl after bowl, wave after wave, an old pattern come round a final time. Foul, painful sores break out, the way sores once broke out on a stubborn nation's cattle and its own skin. The sea turns to blood, like the blood of a dead man, and every living thing in it dies, as a river once turned to blood, undrinkable, its fish dead and floating. The rivers and springs turn to blood too, and an angel of the waters says: you are just, for they poured out the blood of saints and prophets, and you have given them blood to drink; it is what they deserve. The sun is given power to scorch people with fire, and they curse God rather than repent - the same refusal to turn that once outlasted every plague sent against the same stubbornness. The beast's throne is plunged into darkness, and people gnaw their tongues in pain, a darkness that can be felt exactly as it once could.

The great river is dried up, to prepare the way for the kings from the sunrise - a highway made through the water the way one was once made through a sea and, centuries later, through another river's flood-flow. And out of the mouths of the dragon and the beast and the false prophet come three unclean spirits like frogs, gathering the kings of the whole world to the great day of battle, to the place called Armageddon - the field below the mountain where kings had gathered for slaughter before, more than once, and always for the same result.

They come certain of victory. They are already finished. They just do not know it yet.

## The harlot (Ch. 17-19) {#harlot}

One deception still waits to be unmasked before the story can end, and unmasking it reveals what this was always about: not simply defeating evil, but God binding himself to his people forever, like a marriage - the oldest metaphor there is for the oldest covenant there is, now paid off in full.

Here is the deception: a woman seated on many waters, dressed in purple and scarlet, glittering with gold and jewels and pearls, a golden cup in her hand full of abominations and the impurities of her fornication, and on her forehead a name written, a mystery: Babylon the great, mother of prostitutes and of earth's abominations - the same charge once brought against a city called a whoring woman, decked in embroidered linen and oil and honey that were never hers to begin with, trading on gifts she had turned into wages. Kings have committed fornication with her; the merchants of the earth have grown rich from the power of her luxury.

Pull the purple back, though, and there is the beast underneath, seven heads and ten horns, and she rides it as though she rode a servant rather than a monster she cannot control. She is no bride. She is a harlot, drunk not with wine but with the blood of the saints and the blood of the martyrs of Jesus - and plenty inside the Church itself have fallen for the act, the way a people were warned, again and again, not to go whoring after gods that were never gods.

Her final performance looks flawless: I sit as a queen, she says, I am no widow, I will never see grief - the very words once put in the mouth of a virgin daughter of Babylon just before her fall, sit in the dust, uncover your legs, your nakedness will be exposed. It happens exactly that way. The ten horns and the beast that carried her turn instead and hate the harlot, and make her desolate and naked, and devour her flesh and burn her up with fire - the devil's own weapon, turned at last against its master, exactly the way one power has always eventually devoured the last one it used.

Then a marriage supper is announced, the way a feast on a mountain was once promised for all peoples, rich food and well-aged wine, and death itself swallowed up forever. Blessed are those invited to the wedding supper of the Lamb.

The road is finally clear to finish him for good.

## The millenium (Ch. 20) {#1000y}

It is done, even if you never saw it happening. An angel comes down from heaven with the key of the abyss and a great chain, and binds the dragon, that ancient serpent, the devil and satan, for a thousand years, and throws him into the abyss and shuts and seals it over him - the same abyss that once had to be sealed to keep the deep itself in its place.

Thrones are set, and judgment is given to those seated on them, and the souls of those beheaded for the testimony of Jesus and for the word of God come to life and reign with Christ a thousand years - a first resurrection, though no one watching from below could have called it that. All along, while it looked as though the Church was only losing, she was ruling, in the same hidden way a valley of dry bones once had breath enter it before anyone could see it happen: I will put breath in you, and you will come to life.

When the thousand years end, the dragon is released for a little while, and goes out to deceive the nations at the four corners of the earth - Gog and Magog - to gather them for battle, their number like the sand of the seashore, the very phrase once used for a promise about a single man's descendants, turned here into the number of an army marching against them. They march up over the broad earth and surround the camp of the saints and the beloved city, and fire comes down from heaven and consumes them, exactly as fire once fell to settle exactly this kind of argument before.

The devil is thrown into the lake of fire and sulfur, where the beast and the false prophet already are, and they are tormented day and night forever and ever.

Then the great white throne, and the one seated on it, from whose presence earth and sky flee and no place is found for them. The dead, great and small, stand before the throne, and books are opened, and another book is opened, the book of life, and the dead are judged by what is written in the books, according to what they have done - the same court, the same opened books, once seen by a man in a vision on a riverbank, before he was told to shut up the words and seal the book until the time of the end. The time of the end has come. Death and Hades give up their dead and are themselves thrown into the lake of fire - the second death - and anyone whose name is not found written in the book of life is thrown in with them.

## The new heaven and new earth (Ch. 21-22) {#paradise}

And then: a wedding. A new heaven and a new earth, the first heaven and the first earth having passed away, and the sea - the old restless deep, the churning source of every beast that ever rose to devour a people - is no more. A holy city, new Jerusalem, comes down out of heaven from God, dressed like a bride adorned for her husband, and a loud voice from the throne says what everyone has been waiting the whole story to hear: Look - the dwelling place of God is with humankind, and he will dwell with them, and they will be his peoples, and God himself will be with them and be their God. He will wipe every tear from their eyes; there will be no more death, no mourning, no crying, no pain, because the former things have passed away.

The city itself is almost too much to describe: a wall of jasper, the city itself pure gold, clear as glass, its foundations adorned with every kind of jewel there is, twelve gates and each one carved from a single pearl. There is no temple anywhere in it, because the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb are its temple - the whole architecture of tent and courts and veil, finished and folded away, because the thing it always pointed toward has finally arrived. There is no sun and no moon, because they are simply not needed anymore: the glory of God is its light, the Lamb is its lamp - the sun will no longer be your light by day, nor the moon your brightness by night, but the Lord will be your everlasting light - and the gates never close, because there is no more night left to shut them against, and the nations will walk by its light.

Through the middle of it runs a river of the water of life, clear as crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb, and on either bank a tree of life bearing twelve kinds of fruit, yielding its fruit every month, its leaves for the healing of the nations - the same tree once guarded by cherubim and a sword turning every way, no longer guarded from anyone, and the same river once measured ankle-deep, then knee-deep, then a river no one could cross, flowing out from under a threshold to make the salt sea fresh and everything live wherever it goes.

The curse - the one first spoken over cursed ground and thorns, all the way back in a different garden - is gone for good. There will be no more curse. God's people will see his face at last, wear his name on their foreheads, and reign with him forever and ever.

The bride and the groom, together at last, for good. And now that you know the story - go and live like you believe it.
