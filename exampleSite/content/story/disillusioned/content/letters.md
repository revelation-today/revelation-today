The first thing this enormous figure does is dictate letters - to seven small congregations
in seven unremarkable towns. Each one addressed by name. Each one seen exactly as it is.

Every letter starts the same way: *I know.* Before any correction, that. He knows what it has
cost, and how tired they are, and what they are up against.

Then the assessments, and they do not run the way you would expect. Two churches are told
nothing is wrong - the poorest one, and the weakest one. The one in real danger is the
comfortable one that believes it needs nothing.

If you have left a church, or are still in one and cannot say why, read those seven again.
Jesus's own audit of churches is harder than yours. He names the compromise, the drift, the
performance, the deadness behind the busy front. He does it without walking out.

The last letter ends with him outside a door, knocking. Remember who is inside: not
outsiders. His own people, too occupied to notice he is not in the room.

He does not break the door down. He knocks. That is either the most patient thing in the book
or the most frustrating, depending on the day you are having.
