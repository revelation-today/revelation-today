Before the first trumpet, something strange: silence in heaven for about half an hour.

The room that has been singing without pause since chapter four stops. The prayers of God's
people are being brought to the throne, and everything goes quiet to listen.

You can do what you like with that. It is what the text says.

Then the trumpets, and disaster in thirds. A third of the sea, a third of the rivers, a third
of the sun. A third - not everything. These are warnings, deliberately limited, because a
warning is aimed at someone who can still act.

And at the end comes the sentence this whole section is built around: the rest of humanity did
not repent.

They saw it. They did not change. Not because they could not.

If you have watched people look straight at the damage they are doing and carry on - if that
is a large part of why you stopped expecting improvement - the book is not going to tell you
that you misread them. It says the same thing you concluded, and it does not soften it.

What it will not concede is that this is the last word. The warnings are limited on purpose.
Limiting them is not weakness. It is time being deliberately left on the clock.
