A woman in purple and gold holding a golden cup full of filth, riding the beast from chapter
thirteen. She tells the kings of the earth what to do, and she profits from human suffering.

She is not a person. She is every arrangement that looks magnificent and runs on other
people's pain.

Here is how she falls, and it is the strangest turn in the book. God does not send an army.
**The beast and the kings she was riding turn on her and destroy her themselves.**

Evil eats itself. God's plan for her destruction consists largely of letting her be what she
is.

If you have spent years waiting for someone to come and break the machine that grinds people
down, this is not the answer you were expecting. The book says the machine is self-consuming,
and its own operators will finish the job.

Then the kings and merchants stand at a distance and weep - and listen to what they actually
say. It is all about lost trade. Nobody mourns *her*. They mourn their margins. That is all
she was ever worth to them, and she never noticed.

And then, finally, a wedding, because with her gone the real thing can happen. The bride is
ready in fine linen, which the text stops to explain: the linen is the good that God's people
actually did. Not sentiment - deeds.

Heaven opens and a rider comes out on a white horse. Two details. His robe is dipped in blood
*before the battle begins* - it is his own. And he wins alone, with a sword that comes out of
his mouth. He speaks, and it is over.
