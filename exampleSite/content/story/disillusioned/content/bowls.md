Seven bowls - and here a word has to be repaired, because it has probably been used against
you.

God's wrath is not him losing his temper. **It is his refusal to stop before the thing is
actually fixed.** A doctor who will not go home while the patient is unstable. A parent who
will not stop searching. Passion aimed at a problem, that does not let go.

And it does not fall indiscriminately. Chapter sixteen names its targets precisely: the mark
of the beast - everyone who obeyed out of fear and began to act like what they feared; the
fact that God's witnesses suffered for living like Jesus, which God has not forgotten;
hardness, twice over, the refusal to admit being wrong; and finally the gathering with Satan
at Armageddon.

The hardness is the part that makes a room go quiet. It becomes unbearably hot, then
unbearably dark, and people bite their tongues from the pain - and still do not change their
minds. Three times the text says it: they did not repent.

Not because they could not.

They gather at Armageddon convinced they are fighting evil. Satan told them so. Being certain
you are on the right side is not the same as being on it.

Then the seventh bowl is poured, and a voice from the throne says one word: **It is done.**

Not "we won the battle." Done. The same word Jesus said on the cross.
