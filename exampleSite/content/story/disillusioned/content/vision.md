An old man on a bare island, put there because he would not say Caesar was Lord.

Look at the scoreboard. Jesus rose sixty years ago, and Rome still runs the world. The church
is small and frightened. John's friends are dead and he is on a rock. If you had been asked
that year who was winning, you would not have said the Christians - and you would not have
been cynical. You would have been reading the evidence.

Then the sky opens. A voice like a trumpet, and someone standing among seven lampstands: eyes
like fire, feet like bronze out of a furnace, a face too bright to look at. John does not
worship. He falls down like a dead man.

A hand comes onto his shoulder. *Do not be afraid. I am the First and the Last. I was dead,
and look - I am alive for ever, and I hold the keys of death.*

Notice what he is not told. Not that he was wrong about Rome. Not that the exile was a
misunderstanding, or that it was secretly fine. He is told who holds the keys.

That is the shape of every answer in this book. It does not correct your reading of the
evidence. It tells you the room is bigger than the evidence.
