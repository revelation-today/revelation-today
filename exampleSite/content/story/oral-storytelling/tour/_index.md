*A script for one narrator and a gathered group. The group's answer never changes - teach it once, and they will call it back before you finish asking. Practice it now: you ask "Who sits on the throne?" - they answer "The Lamb who was slain!" Good. Begin.*

## How everything started

**Narrator:** In the beginning, there was nothing. Then - breath. Light broke over the water. God knelt in the dust, shaped a man with his own hands, and breathed into him. *(pause here, let the silence sit)* His name was Adam. God gave him a garden and said: this is yours - tend it, rule it.

One thing was not good: Adam was alone. So God built Eve from Adam's own side. For one shining moment, everything was as it should be.

It did not last. The man and the woman wanted what God had given more than they wanted God himself. One small reach. One small bite. The world cracked like a dropped jar.

Driven from the garden, their children built their own gardens instead - cities, towers, kingdoms - anything to fill the hole where God used to be. But God did not let go. He called Abraham: leave everything, follow me. Abraham said yes - even when it cost him the son he waited a lifetime for. Out of Abraham, God grew a whole nation: Israel.

## The promise

**Narrator:** Israel carried the promise like a lamp. Would she love the lamp more than the light? She did. Generation after generation, the people drifted, until the covenant lay in ruins and they were driven from their own land - just like Adam, driven from his garden.

But God was not finished. He promised a Redeemer - one who would build a kingdom that never falls, its door open to anyone willing to walk through.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

## The vision (Ch. 1) {#vision}

*Teach the refrain before you start, if the group has not heard it yet: you ask "Who sits on the throne?" - they answer "The Lamb who was slain!"*

**Narrator:** Picture an old man. Alone. On a rocky island out in the sea. His name is John, and he is there for one reason only: he would not bow to anyone but Jesus.

It is a Sunday, a morning like any other - *(pause)* - until a voice behind him cracks like a trumpet blast, and nothing is ever the same again.

He turns. *(turn your own head, slowly, and let them turn with you)* And there it is - seven lamps of gold burning in the air. And standing among them: someone impossible to describe, impossible to look away from.

Eyes like fire. Feet like bronze pulled glowing from the furnace. A voice like a waterfall roaring. A face too bright to look at - like staring straight into the sun. Seven stars rest in his hand. A sharp sword comes from his mouth.

John does not stay on his feet. He falls down like a dead man.

*(soften your voice now)* Then - a hand on his shoulder. Words that melt the fear before it even finishes forming: "Do not be afraid. I am the First and the Last. I died - and look! - I am alive forever. I hold the keys of Death and the Grave itself."

The kingdom you have been waiting for has already come. And you - yes, you, sitting right there - you are a king and a priest in it.

But one thing must be said before we celebrate. Those seven lamps are the Church - and she does not burn on her own fuel. She burns because he stands among her, holding her alight. Nothing more is asked of her. Nothing less will do.

So I ask you plainly: is she ready?

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

## The letters to the churches (Ch. 2-3) {#letters}

**Narrator:** Seven churches. Seven letters. Seven mirrors, held up to seven rooms full of people just like us. *(hold up seven fingers, and fold one down as you name each church)*

Ephesus works herself to the bone. Correct in every teaching. Faithful in every duty. And somewhere along the way, love slipped through her fingers like water. Without love, all that labor is worth nothing at all.

Smyrna is poor. Hated. Squeezed on every side. And she is one of only two churches Jesus has nothing but praise for.

Pergamum stands her ground in the shadow of Satan's own throne - some die for it - yet false teaching has crept in the back door, quiet as a thief.

In Thyatira, the rot starts at the top: the leaders themselves have wandered off the path.

Sardis looks alive. *(shake your head slowly)* But underneath, she is already dead - so sure she can manage fine without God, and failing at almost everything because of it.

Philadelphia is the other church Jesus praises without holding anything back - small, worn thin, nothing left in the purse, and still faithful to the end.

And then - Laodicea. Lukewarm. Comfortable. Blind to how poor she really is. The only church that gets no praise at all.

*(soften your voice - this is not a door slamming shut)* But even here, Jesus does not walk away. He knocks. He warns. He still wants in.

Seven letters. Seven chances to wake up. And every single one of them is still being read out loud, even now, in rooms just like this one.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** He is the one writing every one of these letters - and he is not finished writing yours.

## Worship (Ch. 4-5) {#worship}

**Narrator:** So it falls to us - to all of us - to get up and get ready for what is coming. But how do you prepare for something this big?

Jesus says: look up.

*(gesture upward, slowly)* The answer is not a plan, not a strategy. The answer is a throne room. Heaven opens, and there it is - the one place where everyone and everything finds its true position before God. The door stands open. It has always stood open.

But do not think worship is an easy fix. It costs something. It means laying your own crown down in the dust, and giving God every part of your life, without exception.

And in that room, one figure stands out from all the rest. Not a lion, though a lion was announced. Look again. *(pause)* A Lamb. Standing as if it had been slain.

*(drop your voice on "slain," then let it rise)*

He is the hero of this whole story. Worthy - when no one else could be found worthy - to open what needed opening, and to rule what needed ruling.

Once you follow him through that door, there is no going back to who you were before.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** Say it once more, and mean it -

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** Hold onto that. Everything that comes next in this story - all of it - is seen from right here, from this room, from this throne. Nothing that follows can shake what you have just seen.

## The seals (Ch. 6) {#seals}

**Narrator:** The seals break. One. By one. And each one lets something loose into the world. *(count them off on your fingers as you go)*

First - a white horse gallops out. A rider with a bow. A crown handed to him. Already hungry for conquest. For one heartbeat, it looks like victory.

It is not. Jesus is not the only one asking for your loyalty, and this rider is a fraud - he has stolen the color of the true King's horse to sell you a lie.

Strip the costume off, and violence is all that is left. Behind the white horse comes a blood-red one, and peace is torn off the earth like a scab. Behind the red horse, a black one, its rider holding a pair of scales: a day's wages for a day's bread, while oil and wine stay safe for those who can still afford them. And behind the black horse - a horse the color of ash. Its rider's name is Death. The grave rides right behind him.

Conquest becomes war. War becomes famine. Famine becomes death.

*(let this land heavy)* Then a seal breaks, and it shows not armies, but an altar - and underneath it, the souls of those killed for staying loyal, crying out with one voice: how long, Lord, holy and true, until you judge the earth and pay back our blood?

They are told: wait. Just a little longer.

The sky itself answers next. The sun goes black. The moon turns to blood. The stars fall like fruit shaken loose from a tree. The sky peels back like an old scroll rolling shut.

The world is drowning in what it has done to itself. And the Church stands right there in the wreckage, crying out too.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** Hold that answer close. It is about to be tested.

## The army of God (Ch. 7) {#army}

**Narrator:** *(this is the turn - let your voice rise with hope)* Before it gets any worse - something happens. Something no one expected.

God's people are sealed. Marked. Claimed, right in the middle of the storm, as unmistakably his own.

This is the army of God! *(let the people feel that this is good news)* And she fights like no army you have ever seen. Look for her swords - she has none. Look for her spears - she has none. She carries no borrowed violence at all.

What does she carry, then?

Worship that will not quit. And loyalty that outlasts everything the enemy can throw at her.

*(slow down here)* A great multitude, too many to count, from every nation, every tribe, every people, every language, standing before the throne and before the Lamb, wearing white, waving palm branches, calling out together with one voice.

Whatever comes next in this story - whatever storm still has to break - one thing is already settled, right now, before it even starts: you know whose side you are on.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** Say it again, and let it become armor -

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** That is the whole army, right there. Not a sword between them. Just people who know whose side they are on, and will not let go of it.

## The trumpets (Ch. 8-9) {#trumpets}

**Narrator:** Now it is the Church's turn to shake the world. Not with weapons - the way Jesus always worked, through what looks like weakness.

*(mime holding something rising in your open palms)* Prayer rises like incense smoke, up before the throne of God. And when it reaches him - the trumpets sound, one after another, seven of them, and the earth itself begins to tremble.

Food fails. Water turns bitter. Trade collapses. Security disappears like morning mist. Everything people leaned on instead of God begins to buckle under them, one trumpet at a time. Surely now, surely now, people will finally look up and ask what is real?

*(pause. shake your head slowly.)*

They do not.

Even as their idols turn on them - even as those same idols strip away every last shred of hope, until death itself starts to look like relief - people cling tighter. Not looser. Tighter.

*(let this sit - do not rush past it)* Hard times alone have never been enough to change a human heart. Not then. Not now. Not ever. A person can lose everything - food, water, safety, comfort - and still cling to the very thing that is destroying them, rather than let it go and turn to God.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** Remember that answer well - because judgment alone was never going to be what turns the world around. Something else is coming. Something sweeter, and harder to swallow.

## The small scroll (Ch. 10) {#scroll}

**Narrator:** The first strike has failed. Trumpet after trumpet shook the earth, and still no one turned back to God. Hard times can crack open just about anything you can name - except a heart that has already set itself on the wrong thing.

*(hold out both hands, as if holding a small scroll)* So a scroll appears. A small one, already open, in a mighty angel's hand - one foot on the sea, one foot on the land, as if to say: this word covers everything, all of it, land and sea alike. And a voice tells John: take it. Eat it.

He eats it. And it is sweet in his mouth, like honey.

*(pause)* Then it turns bitter in his stomach.

That is exactly how this works. Something in this scroll will change everything - though it will take time to digest, time to work its way all the way through him before it comes back out as words for others to hear.

This is not another disaster raining down from the sky. This is a revelation. Something true about God himself, breaking through where judgment alone could never reach - because judgment can break a life open, but only truth, held close and swallowed whole, can heal it.

Sweet going in. Hard to carry once it is inside you.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** That is the truth this whole scroll is made of - sweet to hear, and hard to live out. Chew on it. It is about to change everything that happens next.

## The two witnesses (Ch. 11) {#witnesses}

**Narrator:** Step past the curtain now, and see both worlds as they truly are. The temple is measured, marked out - not because you can earn your way into it with good behavior, but because it is where your loyalty either shows itself plainly, or it does not.

Two witnesses stand in the middle of a hostile city and testify. Dressed in sackcloth. Holding nothing back. Giving everything they have got, for as long as they are given to give it.

And the world kills them for it. *(let your voice drop low)* Kills them - and then throws a party over their bodies, laughing, celebrating, sending each other gifts, glad to be rid of them at last.

For three and a half days, it looks like total defeat. It looks finished.

*(let the silence hang here - count it out: three... two... one...)*

Then - they stand up.

*(lift your voice sharply - let this land like a shock)*

On their feet again, breath in their lungs, and the whole watching world goes cold with fear.

That is exactly the pattern the Church herself is asked to live out, in every generation, in every place she is hated for her testimony. Not a victory that skips over the suffering. Not a shortcut around the cost. A victory that walks straight through it, all the way through, and comes out standing on the other side, alive, unashamed, and still speaking.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** He died too. And he stood up too. Whatever they do to you for staying loyal - that is not the end of your story either.

## The victory of Jesus (Ch. 12) {#jesus}

**Narrator:** Now the same story is told again - pulled back further, so you can see the frame around it. A very different Christmas story, played out across the sky itself.

*(gesture upward, wide)* A woman appears, clothed with the sun, the moon under her feet, twelve stars circling her head like a crown. She is pregnant, crying out in the pain of labor.

Then the sky tears open again, and something far worse appears: an enormous red dragon. Seven heads, crowned. Ten horns. A tail vast enough to sweep a third of the stars out of heaven and hurl them down to the earth.

He plants himself right in front of the woman. Waiting. *(lean in, drop your voice)* The instant the child is born, he means to swallow him whole.

The child is born anyway - a son who will rule every nation. And before the dragon's jaws can close around him, the boy is snatched up, safe, all the way to God's own throne.

War breaks out in heaven! Michael and his angels against the dragon and his -

**All respond:** And the dragon loses!

**Narrator:** There is no place left for him in heaven. He is hurled down to the earth - beaten by the blood of the Lamb, and by the plain testimony of people who would not save their own lives to escape him.

Furious, and running out of time, he turns his rage on the woman - and when she slips out of his reach, he turns it on everyone who belongs to her instead.

*(reassure them - warm your voice)* But take heart anyway. Defeating the dragon was never your job. Jesus already did that, in heaven, before it ever reached you.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** Your job is simpler: keep drawing people toward him, even while the dragon rages harder than ever, because he knows exactly how little time he has left.

## The dragon and the two beasts (Ch. 13) {#beasts}

**Narrator:** Look at the dragon's scoreboard so far. *(hold up one finger for each item)*

He tried to kill Jesus - and failed.

He tried to keep his place in heaven - and was thrown out.

He tried to wipe out Israel - and failed again.

The dragon roared. The dragon lost. The dragon always loses.

*(shift your tone - now he is scrambling, out of good moves)* So he reaches for new weapons. Two beasts - one clawing up out of the sea, one crawling up out of the earth. One rules by brute force, demanding worship at the point of a sword. One deceives with wonders and signs, so dazzling that people forget to ask who is really behind them. And together, they are a grotesque copy - of God, of Christ, of the Spirit - close enough to fool almost anyone who is not paying attention.

But look closer. *(narrow your eyes, lean in)* The imitation is already cracking. The beast conquers only by violence. It dazzles only by trickery. It builds nothing that will last.

Because the One it is copying has already won. Permanently. There is no copy of a Lamb who was slain and is alive forever - there is only the real thing, and everything else is a forgery wearing his face.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** Not the beast. Never the beast. Say it again, so the forgery cannot fool you -

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

## The two harvests (Ch. 14) {#harvest}

**Narrator:** Jesus already won it at the cross. And the Church has kept faith with that victory ever since. Now the enemy's forces scatter like smoke before wind.

*(gesture as if standing on still water)* On a sea of glass and fire, the faithful stand and sing a new song, a song no one else can learn, because no one else has walked the road they walked to get there. Listen for what qualified them - it was not cleverness, not wealth, not power, not even how much they suffered. Their only qualification was staying loyal, all the way through, to the very end.

Not everyone gets to sing. *(let your voice sober)* The ones who played it safe, who sided with the beast to avoid the cost of standing with the Lamb - they now face a far heavier one than the one they were trying so hard to avoid.

Two sickles swing across the earth. *(mime a sweeping motion, twice)*

One harvest gathers in.

One harvest is for judgment.

This is the moment truth stops being theoretical. This is the moment it becomes real - for everyone, no exceptions.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** He is the one gathering the harvest, with his own hand on the sickle. Which one you are gathered into was never a mystery, and it was never decided at the last minute - it was decided by who you stayed loyal to, all along, every ordinary day before this one.

## The bowls of wrath (Ch. 15-16) {#bowls}

**Narrator:** Now God's zeal pours out like something long held back and finally released. *(mime tipping a bowl, again and again)* Bowl after bowl. Seven of them, one after another, wave after wave, until the kingdom built on lies has nowhere left to stand.

Every bowl lands exactly where it is aimed - at false worship, at cruelty toward God's people, at hearts too hardened to break even now, even after everything that came before it: the seals, the trumpets, the warnings, every chance already given and already refused. Nothing is wasted, and nothing is random.

*(gather your voice, building)* The armies of the earth gather for one last stand, drawn together as if by some invisible hand, at a place whose very name has become a byword for disaster.

Armageddon.

They come certain of victory. Banners flying. Weapons sharpened. Certain.

*(pause - almost a whisper)*

They are already finished. They just do not know it yet.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** Not the armies. Not the beast. Not the lie dressed up as a kingdom, with its banners and its promises and its borrowed crown. Only the Lamb. He has already decided how this ends, and no army on earth, however large, however certain, can change the verdict.

## The harlot (Ch. 17-19) {#harlot}

**Narrator:** One deception still waits to be unmasked before the story can end. And unmasking it shows what this whole story was always about - not simply defeating evil, but God binding himself to his people forever. Like a marriage.

Here is the deception. *(gesture as if admiring fine clothing)* A woman, dazzling, draped in jewels that should have belonged to the bride. She promises prosperity to anyone who will drink from her cup.

Pull back the dress, though. *(rip the gesture away suddenly)* There is the beast underneath. Teeth and all.

She is no bride. She is a harlot, riding on the very beast she claims to have tamed. And plenty of people inside the Church itself have fallen for the act, drinking from that cup without asking what was in it, or who was pouring it.

Her final performance looks flawless. It is not.

*(let this land as a surprise)* The very powers the devil sends to destroy the Church turn instead, and tear the harlot apart with their own hands - his own weapon, used against him, the deceiver deceived by his own creation.

The road is finally clear to finish him for good.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** Not the harlot. Not her jewels, not her cup, not her promises of easy prosperity. Only the Lamb - and he is coming for his bride, the real one, dressed not in stolen jewels but in righteousness given as a gift, at last.

## The millenium (Ch. 20) {#1000y}

**Narrator:** It is done - even if you never saw it happening while it was going on.

*(pause, let that surprise sit)* All along, while it looked like the Church was losing - losing her people, losing her standing, losing every visible fight - she was ruling. Not with swords. Not with armies. Not with any kind of power anyone was watching for. She ruled by staying loyal when it cost her everything, and that hidden reign has shaped the outcome of the whole story.

Think of the ones who came before you - the ones who stayed faithful and never saw the victory with their own eyes. They were not wasting their lives. They were ruling, the whole time, with him.

Now comes the last reckoning. A final judgment. *(slow down, let the weight of this show)* Every name is opened. Every deed is weighed. It is decided, once and for all, who spends eternity with God - and who has chosen, all along, not to.

No one is surprised by the verdict who was paying attention to their own life, and no one is left out who ever truly wanted in.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** He was ruling the whole time - even when it did not look like it. Even when you could not see it. Even now.

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

## The new heaven and new earth (Ch. 21-22) {#paradise}

**Narrator:** And then - a wedding! *(let your voice open up wide here - this is the loud part, let the people cheer)*

A city comes down out of heaven, dressed like a bride on her wedding day. And a voice from the throne says what everyone has been waiting the whole story to hear:

"Look - God's home is now among his people. He will live with them. He will wipe every tear from their eyes."

*(slow down, count these on your fingers)* No more death. No more mourning. No more crying. No more pain. All of that has already passed away.

The city itself is almost too much to describe. Walls of jasper. Streets of gold so pure it looks like glass. Foundations set with every color of jewel. Twelve gates, and each one carved from a single pearl.

There is no temple anywhere in it - because God himself, and the Lamb, are the temple now. There is no sun and no moon, because they are simply not needed anymore: the glory of God is the light, the Lamb is the lamp, and the gates never close, because there is no more night left to shut them against.

*(gesture flowing water)* Through the middle of it runs a river, clear as crystal, flowing straight from the throne of God, with a tree on either bank bearing fruit every month, and leaves for healing every nation that ever bled.

The curse - the one that started all the way back in a different garden, the one this whole story began with - is gone. Gone for good.

God's people will see his face at last. Wear his name. Reign with him forever.

The bride and the groom, together at last, for good. God himself moves in with his people - no more distance left to close.

*(final pause - let it be still)* And now that you know the story - go and live like you believe it.

Ask them one more time -

Who sits on the throne?

**All respond:** The Lamb who was slain!

**Narrator:** And he always will be.
