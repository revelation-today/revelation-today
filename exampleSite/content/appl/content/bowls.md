The armies gather at Armageddon certain of victory. They are already finished - they just don't know it yet. That gap between how certain someone feels and how solid the ground actually is under them is worth sitting with, because it's rarely obvious from the inside.

It's easy to assume this is only about someone else's false confidence. It's worth turning the question back on yourself instead.

Confidence of that kind is not loud. It sounds like a plan that has never been stress-tested, a marriage assumed to be fine because nobody has complained lately, a faith that has not been asked a hard question in years, a body treated as though it will keep saying yes. In each case the certainty is real and the ground underneath is untested - and there is no way to tell the difference from the inside without checking.

Armageddon itself is worth a second look too. The name most likely points to Megiddo, a real place overlooking the Jezreel Valley where real armies fought real battles for centuries, several of them won by unlikely heroes ({{% bible val="Judges 4" link="jdg:4" lang="en" %}}, {{% bible val="2 Kings 9" link="2ki:9" lang="en" %}}). That detail matters: the final conflict isn't pictured as some abstract, otherworldly event happening nowhere in particular, but as the work of the same God who has already won concrete victories in history, doing it again. The armies gather there; the battle itself is described later ({{% bible val="Revelation 19" link="rev:19" lang="en" %}}). Vague dread about "the end of everything" is a very different thing from trusting a God with an actual track record.

The two halves of the chapter belong together. If you are sure and unchecked, its warning is for you. If you are frightened of the future in a formless way, its comfort is for you: the end is not chaos arriving, it is a specific God finishing something he has done before.

Checking is not dramatic. Ask your wife or husband the question you have been assuming the answer to. Put a number on the plan and see whether it survives. Let someone who disagrees with you tell you why, and listen to the end. Take the appointment you have been putting off. Any one of those this week is worth more than another year of feeling certain.

## Questions to reflect on

- Where are you currently as certain as those armies were - confident you're standing on solid ground - without having actually checked what that ground is made of?
- What would it look like this week to test that confidence honestly, rather than waiting to find out the hard way?
- Is your sense of how the story ends built on vague dread about the unknown, or on the concrete, historical track record of a God who has already won real battles before?

## Prayer

God of history, you have won real battles in real places, and you will win the last one. Show me where my confidence rests on ground I have never checked, and replace my vague dread about the end with trust in you. Amen.
