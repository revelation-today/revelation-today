"God's home is now among his people. He will live with them. He will wipe every tear from their eyes." No more death, no more mourning, no more pain - not because it never happened, but because all of it has already passed away. That's where the whole story has been heading from the very first page.

The story itself doesn't end with a moral or a summary. It ends with an instruction: now that you know the story, go and live like you believe it.

Which raises the honest question of what that would change by Friday. Living like you believe it is not mainly a feeling about the future; it is a different relationship to loss. If every tear is going to be wiped away by someone who knows which ones they were, grief does not have to be tidied up or explained away now. You can say that something was terrible and still not be without hope. You can stop trying to make the arithmetic work.

It changes what you are willing to spend, too. People who expect the story to end here hold on to what they have; people who expect this ending can afford to be generous with money, time and reputation, because none of it is the last thing they will have. And it changes what you are willing to face: a diagnosis, an ageing parent, your own body slowing, are not the end of the account.

The ending is genuinely good news, but it isn't automatic for everyone, and it's worth being honest about the other half of these closing chapters. Hell isn't primarily a punishment God inflicts from outside - it's the simple, permanent absence of the relationship with God that heaven is made of, the natural end of a life spent refusing him, made final. That's sobering rather than comforting, and it's meant to be: it's the reason "go and live like you believe it" isn't just a private encouragement for you, but an urgent word for anyone you love who's still choosing distance over relationship.

So finish the book with one concrete thing. Name the loss you have been carrying and say out loud that it will be wiped away, without pretending it was fine. Then think of the person you love who is still at a distance, and do the small, unheroic thing that keeps the door open between you - the message, the invitation, the apology you owe them.

## Questions to reflect on

- If you actually lived today as though this ending were as certain as your own name, what's one thing you'd do differently before the day is over?
- What are you carrying that you have been trying to explain away, which this ending would let you simply grieve?
- Who in your life is still choosing distance from God, and does the reality of that choice's ending change how urgently you'd want to reach them?

## Prayer

God, you will make your home with your people and wipe every tear from our eyes. Let this ending shape how I live today. Wake me from distraction and fear, and give me love and urgency for the people I know who are still keeping their distance from you. Amen.
