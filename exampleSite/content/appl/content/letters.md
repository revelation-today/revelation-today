Seven churches, seven very different situations, and Jesus doesn't send the same letter twice. Ephesus needed to hear that correct doctrine without love is worthless. Smyrna and Philadelphia, poor and squeezed on every side, needed nothing but encouragement. Sardis looked alive and was already dead. Laodicea was the one church that received no praise at all - and the one Jesus still knocked for anyway.

The uncomfortable exercise isn't picking your favourite church. It's noticing which letter you'd actually receive if Jesus wrote to you by name this week, not the one you'd prefer.

Notice how each letter opens. Not with what the church says about itself, and not with its numbers, but with "I know" - I know your deeds, your pressure, your poverty, your reputation. These letters are written from the outside, by someone who has been watching. Laodicea thought it was rich. Sardis had a name for being alive. Smyrna, which felt poor, is told it is rich. In every case the church's account of itself and Jesus' account of it are two different documents.

Which is why guessing is the wrong method here. Read the seven letters straight through once and mark the sentence that stings rather than the one that flatters. Then, if you dare, ask someone who knows you well which of the seven sounds most like you. They usually answer faster than you would like.

Sardis is the hardest case, because nothing there feels wrong. No heresy, no persecution, no scandal - just a reputation outliving the thing it was built on. And the prescription is small and unglamorous: wake up, strengthen what remains, remember what you received at the start, hold on to it. Not a new programme. The return of something that was already there.

Whatever letter lands on you, the ending is the same in all seven: a promise to "the one who conquers", which in this book never means winning, only staying loyal. Laodicea got no praise at all and still got a knock at the door. If you have quietly concluded that you are past being wanted, that door is the part of the chapter you most need to believe.

## Questions to reflect on

- If Jesus wrote your church - or you personally - a letter this week, which of the seven would it sound most like, and what's the one thing it would ask you to do differently?
- Where is your own account of how you're doing different from what someone watching closely would write?
- Laodicea got no praise and still got a knock at the door. Where do you need to believe that's true for you too, rather than assuming you're past being wanted?

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you know my situation better than I do. Speak to me the letter I need, not the one I would prefer, and give me the courage to change what you ask me to change. Thank you that you still knock, even at my door. Amen.
