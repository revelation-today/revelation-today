The harlot is dazzling, draped in jewels that should have belonged to the bride, promising prosperity to anyone who will drink from her cup - and plenty of people inside the church itself fall for the act. She isn't obviously evil. She's obviously appealing, which is the actual danger.

Babylon's whole pitch is that you can have the blessing without the belonging - the reward without the relationship it was meant to come from. That offer still gets made constantly, just rarely with a name tag on it.

You hear it in the promise that the right method will produce the result: the prayer formula, the parenting system, the church model, the investment that cannot fail. You hear it in a faith kept for what it provides - the community, the calm, the moral shape for the children - while the person at the centre of it goes unaddressed for months. None of that announces itself as a betrayal. It feels like being sensible.

What makes the chapter frightening is not her wickedness but her welcome. Babylon in this book is not a persecuting power at this point; she is hospitality, trade and comfort, and the kings and merchants who mourn her are mourning a lost market. When she falls, what they miss is the income. That is the sharp question to ask of anything offering you blessing: what exactly would you be sad to lose if it ended tomorrow?

And the call in the middle of it is not a demand to fight her. It is "come out of her, my people" - a matter of separation rather than combat, of noticing what you are drinking. Since the harlot is dressed in what belongs to the bride, telling them apart takes more than taste. The test is not how good the offer looks but what it asks for: the bride's calling always involves belonging to somebody, and costs something now.

So name the cup being held out to you at the moment - a result, a security, a status - and ask whether the thing it offers can be had without Jesus himself. If it can, you have found Babylon's pitch. Take one step back from it this week, and take one step toward the person the blessing was meant to come with.

## Questions to reflect on

- What's a "cup" being offered to you right now that promises a version of blessing without asking for actual loyalty to Jesus in return?
- If your faith stopped delivering its benefits - the community, the calm, the respectability - what would be left of it?
- How would you tell the difference, in your own life, between the bride's calling and the harlot's offer, if both looked equally attractive this week?

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you want me, not only my wish for blessing. When the cup of easy reward is held out to me, give me the wisdom to see what it costs and the love to stay with you. Make me faithful as your bride. Amen.
