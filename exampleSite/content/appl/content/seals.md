> I looked, and there before me was a white horse! Its rider held a bow, and he was given a crown, and he rode out as a conqueror bent on conquest.
>
> — {{% bible val="Revelation 6:2" link="rev:6,2" lang="en" %}} (NIV)

**Read the full text:** {{% bible val="Revelation 6" link="rev:6" lang="en" %}}

The first rider looks like the rescue: a white horse, a crown, a promise to win. White is the colour Christ himself rides in later in the book. But nobody in this chapter is asked to judge the rider by his horse. They see what comes out behind him: war, then prices that put bread out of reach of the poor while the oil and the wine are kept safe, then death. That is how you recognise him - not by what he promises, but by what follows him.

The same riders still come out in that order. First the promise: the leader who will finally fix things, the deal that will make you safe, the movement that is certain to win, the one decision that will end the worry for good. It looks clean, and it often uses the language of faith. Then the sword: whoever does not cheer becomes the enemy. Then the scales: the cost lands on the people with the least, and the comfortable find their oil and wine untouched. Then the pale horse: lives, friendships and whole communities quietly die off.

Two things in the chapter keep this from becoming despair. None of the riders comes out on his own; each one appears only when the Lamb opens a seal. Their havoc is exposed as part of the Lamb's work, and it does not get the last word. And the fifth seal shows the price: under the altar are those who were killed because they would not ride along, and their cry, "How long?", is the cry of the riders' victims. The wait they are given is real, but so is the white robe.

So the question this chapter puts to you is less "what is promising me victory?" than "what has followed it so far, and who has paid for it?"

This week: name one promise you are leaning on - at work, in politics, in your plans, even in church - and write down what has actually followed it. Ask who pays the price of wheat while the oil and wine stay safe, and if it is someone near you, stand with them in one concrete way. And when you catch yourself cheering for something because it looks like winning, look behind it before you join in.

## Questions to reflect on

- Which promise of winning - security, success, finally being proved right - rides at the front of your life at the moment, and what has come out behind it?
- In the things you support, at work, in politics or in church, who is paying the price of wheat while the oil and wine stay untouched?
- The souls under the altar were killed for not going along. Where does refusing to ride along cost you something, and are you willing to pay it?

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you are the only one worthy to open the seals. Keep me from following a white horse just because it looks like rescue. Open my eyes to what comes behind the promises I lean on, and to the people who pay for them. Make me faithful when going along would be easier, and let me trust you to have the last word. Amen.
