> And when I turned I saw seven golden lampstands, and among the lampstands was someone like a son of man …
>
> — {{% bible val="Revelation 1:12–13" link="rev:1,12-13" lang="en" %}} (NIV)

**Read the full text:** {{% bible val="Revelation 1" link="rev:1" lang="en" %}}

John's vision isn't decoration before the "real" content starts - it's the lens for everything that follows. The church exists to shine, the way seven lampstands shine: not on her own fuel, but held alight by the one standing among them. Nothing more is asked of her. Nothing less will do.

That raises an uncomfortable question about your own week, not just the church in general. Shining on your own fuel looks like performance - effort, image management, running on empty and hoping no one notices.

You can usually spot it by the tiredness that comes with it. Leading something on four hours of sleep and an argument in the car, and singing anyway. Answering a friend's question about faith with sentences you have not personally believed for months. Staying on a rota because the person who asked would be disappointed, not because there is anything left in you to give. None of that is hypocrisy exactly. It is a lamp still giving light while the oil runs out.

Being held alight looks different: quieter, less impressive to watch, but able to keep burning long after your own strength would have run out. The picture is worth taking literally. A lamp produces nothing. Its whole job is to stay where the oil reaches it and let itself be lit - so the question is never "how much more can I manage", but "what am I actually connected to".

This is not an argument for doing less. These churches are commended for hard work, and the one that had stopped working was in real trouble. It is an argument about the supply. Dropping the pretence that the supply is yours is the one thing that will keep you going when the week is long.

So name the inputs. Where does your light actually come from in an ordinary week - one honest conversation, a psalm you read rather than posted, twenty minutes when nothing is asked of you? Put one of them back this week, before the tiredness makes the choice for you. When Jesus asks the church here whether it is ready, he is not asking whether it is impressive. He is asking whether it is lit.

## Questions to reflect on

- This week, has your faith mostly been running on your own fuel, or has it actually been fed by something outside you?
- Where are you keeping up a light that has no oil behind it - and who would you have to disappoint in order to stop?
- If Jesus asked you the same question he's really asking the church here - "are you ready?" - what would your honest answer be right now?

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you stand among the lampstands. Forgive me for trying to shine on my own fuel. Keep me burning with your light, not my performance, so that I am ready when you come. Amen.
