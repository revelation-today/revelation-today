If chapter 20 is right that those who stayed faithful under pressure are already reigning with Christ right now, in a way the world can't see, then the small, unglamorous faithfulness of your own ordinary week - the temptation you didn't give in to, the truth you told when a lie would have been easier - isn't a footnote while you wait for "real" reward later. It already counts as reigning.

That's worth sitting with regardless of which of the three views of the millennium you land on. The debate is about a number; the promise underneath it is the same in every view: nothing done in loyalty to Christ is ever wasted, even when nobody is watching and no one will ever know.

It helps to notice who is reigning in the chapter. Not the people who succeeded. The ones who were executed - beheaded for their testimony, as unimpressive an outcome as the ancient world could produce. Whatever else "reigning with Christ" means, it does not mean visible power, and it is not a reward for winning.

That should change where you look for significance this week. Most of what you will do that matters will be invisible by design: the patience with the same person for the fourth time, the money given without telling anyone, the work done properly when a shortcut would never have been found out, the anger you did not pass on to your children. It is tempting to treat these as maintenance, the boring part of life you get through on the way to something that counts. This chapter calls them the reign.

There is a warning tucked in beside the comfort. The dragon is bound but not gone, and he is released for a short time at the end. Faithfulness now is not a guarantee of an easy later; the story has one more hard stretch in it. The promise is not that nothing will go wrong. It is that nothing already done in loyalty will be lost when it does.

So take one small thing you did this week that nobody noticed - or one you are about to do and were tempted to skip - and treat it as what it is. Not maintenance. The reign, in the only form it takes while the world cannot see it.

## Questions to reflect on

- Where this week did faithfulness cost you something small - and did it feel like it counted for anything at the time?
- What are you treating as maintenance that this chapter would call reigning?
- If you already believed you were reigning with Christ right now, hidden but real, what would you do differently today?

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you that nothing done in loyalty to you is ever wasted, even when no one sees it. Give me faithfulness in the small, ordinary choices of this week, and let me live today as one who already belongs to your reign. Amen.
