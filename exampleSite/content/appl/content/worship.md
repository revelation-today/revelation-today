Heaven's answer to an overwhelming situation isn't a strategy - it's a throne room. But walking through that door costs something specific: laying your own crown down, handing over every part of your life without carving out an exception. Worship that doesn't cost anything usually isn't worship yet, just admiration from a safe distance.

It helps to be concrete about what a crown is. It is whatever you would defend first if someone questioned it: a competence you are known for, a reputation inside a small circle, control of the money, the right to decide how your children turn out, the certainty that in this one area you know best. Crowns are rarely bad things in themselves. That is exactly what makes them hard to put down.

The elders in the vision are not forced to hand theirs over. They do it because of what they can see. The gesture is a conclusion, not a punishment - the natural response to finding the throne already occupied. Worship in this book is never self-hatred. It is the relief of stopping the pretence that the weight of the world is yours to carry.

The Lamb who looked as if he had been slain is the one who makes that exchange worth making. He isn't asking for a crown he hasn't already earned the hard way himself, and he isn't asking you to become incompetent or passive. Handing over the money doesn't mean refusing to budget. Handing over your children doesn't mean giving up on raising them. It means the outcome stops being the thing you must secure at any cost.

So do it in the particular rather than in general. Name the one crown you would least like to be asked about. Say it to God in plain words, as a handover rather than a topic. Then take one step this week that only makes sense if you meant it: the conversation you have been managing, the decision you have been sitting on, the task you have refused to let anyone else do.

General worship costs nothing and changes nothing. The throne room is where the specific thing gets left.

## Questions to reflect on

- What's the one part of your life you'd most want to keep out of that throne room if you're honest - the "crown" you're still quietly holding onto?
- What would it actually look like this week to lay that specific thing down, rather than worshipping in general terms that cost you nothing?
- What are you afraid would happen if you stopped holding that one thing together yourself?

## Prayer

Lamb of God, you are worthy. You know the crown I still hold on to. Help me lay it down before you, not in general words but in this one specific thing, because you gave everything first. Amen.
