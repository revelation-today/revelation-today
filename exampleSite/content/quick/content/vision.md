Revelation opens by saying exactly what kind of book it claims to be: an urgent message from Jesus, sent through a man named John to seven congregations, meant to be acted on at once - not a puzzle filed away for a later generation to decode. "The time is near" is said twice, at the very start and the very end of the book. That framing matters for everything that follows.

Then comes the vision itself: John, exiled on Patmos for refusing to bow to anyone but Jesus, sees the risen Christ standing among seven golden lampstands, holding seven stars, with eyes like fire and a voice like rushing water. John falls down as if dead - and Jesus' first words are "Don't be afraid." He is the one who died and is alive forever, holding the keys of death itself.

The lampstands and stars are then explained inside the text itself: they are the seven churches and their "angels" (messengers) - so the vision is about those churches, and what they are for. The image is simple and says one thing twice: a church exists to give light, the way a lampstand does - and only stays alight from a source outside itself, since a lampstand needs oil and a star shines because it is held in place. Dependence, not self-sufficiency, is the whole point.

Everything Jesus says to the seven churches in the next two chapters will measure them against this one vision: are they actually being this kind of light, in this kind of dependence?

## Key images

- **Alpha and Omega** - the first and last letters of the Greek alphabet: Jesus is the beginning and the end of the story, and he does not change.
