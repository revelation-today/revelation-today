# The Seven Letters
### Revelation 2–3

## 1. School reports

Imagine your teacher wrote a report card for your whole class — not for each person, but for
the class as one thing. What would it say? What are you good at together? What is the thing
everybody quietly knows is a problem?

That is exactly what chapters 2 and 3 are. Seven report cards, for seven real churches, in
seven real towns. And Jesus writes them himself.

## 2. The seven churches, quickly

- **Ephesus** — busy, hard-working, brilliant at spotting people who teach nonsense. But
  somewhere along the way they stopped loving. Everything else about them is fine.
- **Smyrna** — small, poor, in real trouble, some of them in prison. Jesus has no criticism
  for them at all.
- **Pergamum** — surrounded by genuine evil; one of them has already been killed for it. But
  they let people teach wrong things inside the church.
- **Thyatira** — a small town church that is actually growing in faith and has put up with a
  great deal. The trouble is that their own leaders are the ones going wrong.
- **Sardis** — grand, impressive, everything apparently under control. That is the problem:
  they have organised Jesus right out of their lives.
- **Philadelphia** — they try hard, they face opposition, they are genuinely poor. And they
  stay faithful. Jesus has no criticism for them either.
- **Laodicea** — rich, healthy, comfortable, and quite sure they are better than everyone
  else.

## 3. Who got it right?

Ask the children to guess before you tell them.

**Smyrna and Philadelphia.** The two smallest. The two poorest. The two with the most
trouble. They are the only two Jesus finds nothing to correct in.

That is worth sitting with for a moment, because it is not how we usually score things.

## 4. Four ways to go wrong

The other five show four different ways of missing it, and none of them looks dramatic from
the outside:

- **Being loveless** while doing everything else right (Ephesus)
- **Being arrogant** because things are going well (Laodicea)
- **Living as if you don't really need God** (Sardis)
- **Letting wrong ideas about Jesus spread**, and leading others into them (Pergamum,
  Thyatira)

Notice that a church can be busy, correct, popular and well-organised and still be on this
list.

## 5. What Jesus does with all of them

Here is the part that is easy to miss. Jesus does not close a single one of these churches
down. Every letter ends with a promise for "the one who conquers" — which simply means the
one who keeps going.

He tells them the truth, and then he keeps the door open. Both halves matter.

## 6. Our own report card

If Jesus wrote to your church, or your group, or your family — what do you think he would
praise? What would he say gently, but honestly?

The point is not to feel bad. Smyrna and Philadelphia were not the impressive ones. They were
the faithful ones.

**Memory verse:** "Be faithful, even to the point of death, and I will give you the crown of
life." (Revelation 2:10)

## 7. Prayer

Jesus, thank you that you tell us the truth instead of just being polite. Thank you that you
never give up on us. Help us to be faithful even when we are small, and kind even when we are
right. Amen.

## Appendix: Ideas for leaders

**Opening activity:** Write the seven town names on cards and hand them out. As you read each
letter, that child stands. It keeps a long chapter moving.

**Craft idea:** A class report card — "What we are good at" on one side, "What we could grow
in" on the other. Fill it in together, gently. Keep it to look at in a few months.

**Questions:**
1. Why do you think the two churches with the hardest lives got no criticism at all?
2. Can somebody do everything right and still be missing something? What?
3. Which of the seven sounds most like us?

**Song suggestions:** A song about faithfulness; a song about Jesus knowing us.

**Prep note:** Ephesus is the one children find hardest — everything correct, but loveless.
It is worth the extra minute, because it is the trap that catches good, well-taught children.
