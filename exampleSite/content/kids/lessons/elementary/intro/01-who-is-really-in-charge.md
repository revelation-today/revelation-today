# Who Is Really in Charge?
### The world Revelation was written into

> **Story to read:** [The Emperor Who Wanted to Be God]({{< relref "story/elementary/content/background" >}})  
> **Bible:** {{% bible val="Revelation 1:9" link="rev:1,9" lang="en" %}}


> **Bible:** {{% bible val="Revelation 1:9" link="rev:1,9" lang="en" %}}


## 1. The clapping game

Let's try something. Every time I raise my hand, everyone has to stand up, clap, and shout
"You're the greatest!" If you don't do it, you sit out the next round.

*(Play two or three rounds, then stop and ask:)*

- How did that feel after the third time?
- Was it fair that someone had to sit out?
- What if you had to do this every day of your life — even on days when you didn't mean it?

Hold on to that feeling. Something very like it — except completely real — is where the book
of Revelation begins.

## 2. A king who wanted to be a god

When Revelation was written, almost the whole known world belonged to one empire: Rome. At
the top was one man, the emperor.

The emperor's message was simple: *I give you safety. I give you enough to eat. I give you
peace. All I ask is that everyone treats me as the greatest — almost like a god.*

You could see it everywhere. His face was on every coin. Statues of him stood in the market
squares. At festivals, everyone raised a cup and praised him. Anyone who refused stood out
immediately — and standing out could get you into serious trouble.

## 3. The problem for Christians

The Christians looked at all this and thought: *Wait. We already have a King, and his name
is Jesus. Safety, food, a good life — those come from God, not from the emperor.*

So they could not honestly join in the shouting. And that made life hard, because refusing
was not a one-time decision. It came up again and again: at work, at parties, at the market.

## 4. John, who said no

The man who wrote Revelation was called John. He tells us plainly what happened to him: he
would not go along, and he was sent away to a small, lonely island called Patmos.

And there, cut off from everyone, God showed him something enormous — that Jesus is alive
and truly reigns, more than any emperor ever could. Everything John wrote afterwards was
meant to give ordinary Christians the courage to stay with Jesus, whatever it cost.

## 5. Who is our emperor?

Nobody today has to bow to a statue. But the question underneath has not gone anywhere:
**Who do you most want to keep happy?**

- Is it more important that other kids think you're cool, or that you do the right thing?
- Do you feel safer with your phone than with God?
- Have you ever gone along with something you knew was wrong, just to stay in the group?

Those are the emperors of our time. They don't wear crowns, and that's exactly what makes
them easy to miss.

## 6. Nobody decides all at once

Here is the part worth remembering. Nobody wakes up one morning and decides to leave Jesus
behind. It happens in small steps — one excuse, one bit of going along, one moment of
staying quiet — until one day you look up and something else has quietly become the boss.

That's why Revelation keeps showing the same picture over and over. It is trying to help us
notice, before we drift.

## 7. The good news

We don't have to hide, and we don't have to buy our way into belonging. Jesus is already the
true King — stronger than any emperor, any trend, any crowd. He invites us to belong to him
honestly, out in the open.

**Memory verse:** "No one can serve two masters." (Matthew 6:24)

## 8. Prayer

Jesus, thank you that you are the real King, stronger than anything that frightens us and
anything we try to impress. Help us to belong to you honestly, even when it costs something.
Show us when we have quietly started trusting something else. Amen.

## Appendix: Ideas for leaders

**Opening game:** The clapping game above. Let it run just long enough to become tiresome —
the point lands when the children feel the pressure, not when it's explained.

**Craft idea:** Each child draws a crown. On one side, they write or draw the thing that
sometimes acts like an emperor in their week — phone, friends, being funny, winning. On the
other side, "Jesus." Ask which crown they want to be the bigger one.

**Questions:**
1. When is it hardest to say no while everyone else is saying yes?
2. What would you do if someone laughed at you for believing in Jesus?
3. What is sometimes more important to you than Jesus, even when you'd rather it wasn't?

**Song suggestions:** A song naming Jesus as King; a movement song about courage.

**Prep note:** This lesson sets up the whole series. If the children only remember one thing,
make it the question "Who is really in charge?" — every later lesson answers it again.
