# The Vision
### Revelation 1

## 1. A surprising question

Put your hand up if you rule the world.

*(Wait. Let it be awkward.)*

Nobody? Then this book is going to surprise you, because the very first chapter says that you
do — and that you have done for two thousand years.

## 2. What John saw

John is on his lonely island when he hears a voice like a trumpet behind him. He turns
around, and what he sees is so overwhelming that he falls down as if he were dead.

He sees Jesus — but not the Jesus of the Christmas pictures. This Jesus has eyes like fire,
a voice like rushing water, and a face like the sun at midday. He is standing in the middle
of seven golden lampstands.

And the first thing this astonishing figure does is reach out a hand and say: **"Do not be
afraid."**

That matters. The most powerful person in the universe uses his power, first, to calm
somebody down.

## 3. Kings and priests

Then comes the surprising part. John writes that Jesus "has made us a kingdom and priests."

Not *will make.* **Has made.** Already. Two thousand years ago.

So we really are meant to rule — and Revelation is largely a book about *how*. Because
ruling is a serious job, and there is a wrong way to do it.

## 4. How we are supposed to rule

Revelation gives two answers straight away, and the whole book keeps coming back to them.

**First: we depend completely on Jesus.** Without him we cannot rule at all — we can only
push people around, which is not the same thing. Ruling means ruling the way Jesus does.

**Second: we are meant to be light.** The seven lampstands are the seven churches. A lamp
does not shout. It does not force anybody. It simply makes it possible to see. Many people
are living in the dark — sometimes because they are frightened, sometimes because they are
ashamed — and we can carry light into that.

## 5. So what kind of power is this?

Think about the difference between a king in a film and a lamp in a dark room.

The king wins by being stronger than everyone. The lamp wins by being *there*, quietly,
until people can see where they are going. Revelation says the second one is the real power,
and it spends twenty-two chapters proving it.

## 6. The question the book will ask

Next time we meet seven real churches, in seven real towns, and we find out whether they
lived up to any of this.

Some did. Some very much did not. And Jesus is honest with all of them — which is its own
kind of good news, because it means he tells us the truth too.

**Memory verse:** "Do not be afraid. I am the First and the Last." (Revelation 1:17)

## 7. Prayer

Jesus, thank you that the first thing you say is "do not be afraid." Thank you that you have
already made us yours. Teach us to rule the way you do — by serving, and by shining, and not
by pushing. Amen.

## Appendix: Ideas for leaders

**Opening activity:** Darken the room as much as you safely can, then light one candle or
switch on one small torch. Ask what changed. Nobody was forced to do anything — and yet
everything is different. That is the lampstand picture.

**Craft idea:** Seven paper lampstands, one per child if the group is small. Each child
writes on theirs one place where they could be light this week (a person, a playground, a
sibling).

**Questions:**
1. Why do you think Jesus says "do not be afraid" before he says anything else?
2. What is the difference between being the boss and being a light?
3. Where is it dark around you — and could you carry a light there?

**Song suggestions:** A quiet song about Jesus being with us; a lively one about shining.

**Prep note:** Resist explaining the strange imagery in detail. The point of chapter 1 is the
*impression* — overwhelming power, and the first word of that power is comfort.
