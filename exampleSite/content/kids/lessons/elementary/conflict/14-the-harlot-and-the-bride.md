# The Harlot and the Bride
### Revelation 17–19

## 1. Two invitations

Describe two parties. At the first, everyone is beautiful, everything sparkles, and you are
only welcome as long as you are useful. At the second, you are wanted because you are you,
and the host has been looking forward to it for years.

Which is which? These chapters put both side by side deliberately.

## 2. Who is the harlot?

The word sounds strange, and children of this age can handle the plain explanation: in the
Bible it means **someone who looks beautiful on the outside and is cruel underneath**, and
who treats love as a trade.

She is dressed in purple and gold, holding a golden cup — and the cup is full of filth. She
sits on the beast we met in chapter 13. She tells the kings of the world what to do, and she
enjoys people's suffering.

She is not a person. She is every system that looks glorious and runs on other people's pain.

## 3. How she falls

This is the strangest and most striking turn in the book.

God does not send an army. **The beast and the kings she has been riding turn on her and
destroy her themselves.**

Evil eats itself. God's plan for her destruction is, mostly, to let her be what she is.

## 4. The kings crying

Then all the kings and merchants stand at a distance and weep — and if you listen to what
they are actually saying, it is entirely about their lost business. Nobody is sad about
*her*. They are sad about what they can no longer get.

That is what she was always worth to them. She just never noticed.

## 5. And then the wedding

Meanwhile, everyone who belongs to God is celebrating — because now that she is gone, the
real thing can happen. The Lamb's wedding. The bride has made herself ready, and she is
dressed in fine linen, which the text explains: it is the good things God's people have
actually done.

Two women, two cups, two outcomes. The whole book has been building to this comparison.

## 6. The rider

Then heaven opens and Jesus rides out on a white horse to deal with the beast and the kings.

Two details worth noticing. His robe is dipped in blood **before the battle starts** — it is
his own. And he defeats them, entirely on his own, with the sword that comes out of his
mouth. He speaks, and it is over.

**Memory verse:** "Blessed are those who are invited to the wedding supper of the Lamb."
(Revelation 19:9)

## 7. Prayer

Jesus, thank you that you want us for ourselves and not for what we can give you. Help us to
see through things that look beautiful and are cruel underneath. And thank you that you are
preparing a celebration. Amen.

## Appendix: Ideas for leaders

**Opening activity:** The two parties. Ask which one they would rather be invited to, and
why.

**Craft idea:** Two cups drawn side by side — one gold on the outside, one plain. Children
write inside each what it actually holds.

**Questions:**
1. How can something look beautiful and be cruel?
2. Why do you think the kings only cried about their business?
3. What is the difference between being wanted for what you can do and being wanted for who
   you are?

**Song suggestions:** A celebration song; something about Jesus coming.

**Prep note:** If asked what a harlot is, the definition in section 2 is sufficient and true.
There is no need to go further with this age group, and the chapter's own point is about
loyalty bought and sold rather than anything else.
