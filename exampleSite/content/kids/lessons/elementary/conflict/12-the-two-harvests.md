# The Two Harvests
### Revelation 14

## 1. Sorting

Bring a bowl of mixed things — buttons and coins, two colours of pasta — and have the group
sort them quickly.

Sorting is not cruelty. It is just telling what is what. Chapter 14 is a sorting chapter.

## 2. Back to God's army

After chapter 13, which was heavy going, chapter 14 opens with relief: the Lamb is standing
on Mount Zion, and 144,000 are with him — the same army as chapter 7.

And they are singing. A new song that only they can learn.

## 3. Their secret weapon

Ask what the group thinks the secret weapon is. Guesses will involve strength or cleverness.

It is neither. It is that **they stayed faithful.** They kept trusting Jesus and never
pretended not to know him.

That is it. That is the whole weapon. It is available to everybody in the room, which is
rather the point.

## 4. Three angels

Then three angels fly over with three announcements:

- the first has **good news for every nation** — the offer is still open, to everyone
- the second says **Babylon is fallen** — the great fake city is finished
- the third gives a serious warning about following the beast

Notice the order. The good news comes *first*. The warning is real, but the invitation is
what leads.

## 5. Two harvests

Then the harvest, and it comes in two parts, which is why the chapter matters.

**The first** is a gathering-in. People brought home to be with Jesus — some of them, quite
possibly, because they watched somebody like you stay faithful when it was awkward.

**The second** is a winepress. Those who wanted nothing to do with Jesus' blood being given
for them find that there is no other way this can end.

Say it plainly and without relish: this is the sad half, and Revelation does not enjoy it
either.

## 6. Why two?

Because faith is a real choice, and real choices have real outcomes. If nothing at all
depended on it, it would not be a choice — it would be a game.

God has spent fourteen chapters inviting, warning, sealing, protecting and announcing good
news to every nation on earth. Nobody arrives at the winepress by accident.

**Memory verse:** "Here is the patient endurance of the saints who keep God's commands and
remain faithful to Jesus." (Revelation 14:12)

## 7. Prayer

Jesus, thank you that staying faithful is enough — that we don't have to be clever or strong.
Thank you that the good news goes to everyone first. Help us to be the kind of example
someone else gets gathered in behind. Amen.

## Appendix: Ideas for leaders

**Opening activity:** The sorting bowl. Keep it quick and matter-of-fact.

**Craft idea:** A sheaf of paper wheat. Each child writes on one stalk the name of somebody
they would like to see gathered in — and takes it home to pray for them.

**Questions:**
1. Why do you think the only weapon mentioned is staying faithful?
2. Why does the good news get announced before the warning?
3. Who might be watching you and learning something?

**Song suggestions:** A song about faithfulness; a joyful one, since the chapter opens with
singing.

**Prep note:** The winepress imagery is strong. Read section 5 in a level voice, do not
dramatise it, and move promptly to section 6, which is where the meaning is.
