# Elementary Track: Revelation for Ages 8 to 12

This is the **core track** in our Revelation Today teaching series, written for **ages 8 to
12** — children old enough to follow a story across several weeks, to notice when something
doesn't add up, and to ask the awkward question out loud.

It sits between our early-childhood track (ages 4 to 7), which takes one picture per session
and stays there, and our teen track (ages 13 to 18), which handles the genuinely difficult
material directly.

## What these lessons are

Sixteen sessions of about 15 minutes each, working straight through Revelation from the
Roman world John wrote into, to the New Jerusalem at the end. Each session has an opening
activity, the Bible story told plainly, an application, a memory verse, a prayer, and an
appendix of prep ideas for the leader — games, crafts, discussion questions and song
suggestions.

These are plans for **preparing and leading a group**, not pages for quiet personal reading.

## What they are not

There are no timelines to memorise, no dates to calculate, and no attempt to match the
symbols onto today's news headlines. Children of this age are perfectly capable of handling
the book as it is: a letter written to frightened people, telling them that Jesus has already
won and that they can stop being afraid.

Where the book is genuinely strange, we say so. Where grown-ups disagree, we say that too.

## The five parts

1. **Introduction & Background** — the world Revelation was written into, and the opening vision
2. **The Seven Churches** — seven report cards, written by Jesus
3. **Worship, Seals & Trumpets** — the throne room, and what comes out of it
4. **The Great Conflict** — the dragon, the beasts, and why they always lose
5. **The Ending** — the thousand years, and the city where God lives with us

## One thread, all the way through

Every session comes back to the same question, first asked in session one: **who is really in
charge?** Revelation gives the same answer sixteen times, in sixteen different pictures. By
the end, the children should be able to give it themselves.
