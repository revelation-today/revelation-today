# The Little Scroll
### Revelation 10

> **Story to read:** [The Small Scroll]({{< relref "story/elementary/content/scroll" >}})  
> **Bible:** {{% bible val="Revelation 10" link="rev:10" lang="en" %}}


## 1. Eating your homework

Ask: has anyone ever been told to "digest" a book, or to "swallow your pride"? What do those
phrases mean? We use eating as a picture for taking something *in* — properly, all the way.

Keep that, because chapter 10 is going to ask somebody to eat a book. Literally.

## 2. Where we are

Quick recap, because this is a hinge.

The first mystery — the mystery of the church — is finished. We saw Jesus win at the cross,
the church turn up as a surprising kind of army, and prayer strip away people's false
securities. And at the end, people held on to those securities harder than before.

So a second mystery begins: the mystery of God.

## 3. A giant angel and a small scroll

An enormous angel comes down, one foot on the sea and one on the land, and he is holding a
scroll. A little one this time.

Jesus was the one worthy to open the big scroll. This smaller one is for John — and for us.

And John is told to take it and eat it.

## 4. Sweet, then bitter

He does, and it happens exactly as he is warned: sweet as honey in his mouth, and sour in his
stomach afterwards.

That is worth talking about honestly with this age group. God's message *is* sweet — it is
the best news there is. And carrying it is sometimes hard on you. Both are true, and
Revelation refuses to pretend otherwise.

## 5. Making it part of you

Eating means taking something in until it becomes part of you. Not memorising it. Not having
it on a shelf. Not being able to answer questions about it.

You can carry a book in a bag for years and be unchanged by it. You cannot eat something and
stay the same.

## 6. Two things to hold on to

**First: this ends well.** By the end of the mystery of God, Satan's kingdom is finished
completely. We are told the outcome before we are told the story, which is a kindness.

**Second: God is in control the whole way through.** What comes next in this book gets wild,
and we could not handle it without knowing who is holding it.

**Memory verse:** "I took the little scroll and ate it. It tasted as sweet as honey."
(Revelation 10:10)

## 7. Prayer

God, thank you that your words are sweet. Help us not just to know them but to take them in,
until they change how we live. And when carrying them is hard, help us to remember how it
ends. Amen.

## Appendix: Ideas for leaders

**Opening activity:** Honey on a spoon, and something sour — lemon, or a sour sweet — one
after the other, if allergies allow. Nothing explains 10:10 faster.

**Craft idea:** Small paper scrolls. Each child writes one verse or promise on theirs, rolls
it up, and takes it home in a pocket rather than a bag — the point being to keep it close.

**Questions:**
1. What is the difference between knowing something and taking it in?
2. Can good news ever be hard to carry? When?
3. Why do you think God tells us the ending before he tells us the middle?

**Song suggestions:** A song about God's word; something about trusting the ending.

**Prep note:** This is a short chapter and a short lesson. If you have time left, use it on
the honey-and-lemon activity rather than adding material — the physical memory is the point.
