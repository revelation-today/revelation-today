# God's Army
### Revelation 7

## 1. Draw an army

Give everyone thirty seconds to draw an army. Then look at what they drew.

Weapons? Armour? Lots of people who look angry?

Now let's see God's.

## 2. First, the marking

Before anything else happens, God does something for his people: he seals them. A mark, to
show whose army they are in.

We are not told exactly what it looks like, and that is fine. What matters is what it means:
*God has already noticed you, and he is watching out for you.* That becomes important later,
when things get hard — everyone else is frightened and discouraged, and God's people still
have hope. Nobody can take that off them.

## 3. Then, the counting

Then the army is counted, because in a good army nobody gets lost.

John *hears* the number: 144,000. Try picturing that — you can't, really. It's a stadium and
a half.

Then John *looks* — and sees a crowd so large that nobody can count it at all.

(This happens twice in Revelation now: he hears one thing and sees another, and the seeing
is bigger. Watch for it.)

## 4. What this army is actually like

Here is where the drawings from the start turn out to be wrong.

- **They are worshippers.** They are not trying to hurt anybody. They want to do people good
  and invite them to Jesus.
- **They have come out of the great tribulation** — which is not a week in the future, but
  the whole long stretch since Jesus came, in which God's people go through hard things
  without deserving them. Sometimes people dislike us simply for belonging to Jesus.
- **They do not hold grudges.** That is the actual weapon. Love outlasts.

## 5. What weapons do we have?

Ask the children directly: if you are in this army, what are you armed with?

Revelation's answer, gathered from across the book: staying faithful, telling the truth,
refusing to hate back, and prayer — which turns out to be the strongest of the lot, as we
will see next time.

## 6. The comfort at the end

Chapter 7 ends with one of the kindest sentences in the Bible: God "will wipe every tear from
their eyes."

Not *there were never any tears.* The tears were real. They get wiped.

**Memory verse:** "God will wipe away every tear from their eyes." (Revelation 7:17)

## 7. Prayer

Thank you, God, that you have marked us as yours and that nobody can undo it. Thank you that
your army fights by loving. Help us not to hate people back. And thank you that one day you
will wipe our tears yourself. Amen.

## Appendix: Ideas for leaders

**Opening activity:** The thirty-second army drawing. Keep the drawings and come back to them
at section 4 — the contrast does the teaching for you.

**Craft idea:** Each child designs a "seal" — a small mark or badge showing they belong to
God. Stickers on hands work well for younger children in the group.

**Questions:**
1. What surprised you about God's army?
2. What is the difference between winning a fight and outlasting somebody?
3. Has anyone ever been unkind to you for something that wasn't your fault?

**Song suggestions:** A song about God's protection; a gentle one about comfort.

**Prep note:** If a child asks whether the 144,000 is a literal number, the honest answer is
the one the text gives — he *heard* 144,000 and *saw* an uncountable crowd. Let both stand.
