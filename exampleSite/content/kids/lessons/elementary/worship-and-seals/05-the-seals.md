# The Seals
### Revelation 6

> **Story to read:** [The Seals]({{< relref "story/elementary/content/seals" >}})  
> **Bible:** {{% bible val="Revelation 6" link="rev:6" lang="en" %}}


## 1. Spot the fake

Show two things that look similar — a real coin and a chocolate one, a photo and a drawing of
it. How do you tell? Usually you have to look for a while, or use it, before the fake gives
itself away.

Chapter 6 is about somebody being found out.

## 2. Someone who wanted the scroll

Jesus is the one worthy to open the scroll, and now he starts.

But there is someone who would badly have liked to hold that scroll himself: Satan. And here
is the thing — plenty of people do not think of him as the bad one at all. They think of him,
if they think of him, as exciting, or strong, or free.

The four horsemen are how that gets found out.

## 3. Four seals, four steps

Watch what happens across the first four seals. It is a sequence, not four random disasters.

- **First seal** — a rider goes out looking magnificent, like a conquering hero. This is the
  advertisement.
- **Second seal** — the same power turns out to take peace away. He starts fights.
- **Third seal** — now there is hunger. People are going without.
- **Fourth seal** — and at the end of the road: death.

Four steps, and the mask is off. What looked heroic at the start is revealed for what it
always was.

## 4. But it hurts

That is genuinely useful — now everyone can see it. But there is a cost, and Revelation does
not hide it: the people who *already* knew are suffering in the meantime.

So at the fifth seal they cry out: "How long?"

And notice what Jesus does not say. He does not say "stop complaining." He says, in effect,
*you are right.* They are given white robes and told to wait a little longer.

Being told you are right about something being wrong is not nothing.

## 5. The sixth seal

Then comes the sixth seal, and the whole world is shaken at once. Everyone — kings, generals,
everyone — is completely overwhelmed.

They had not expected Jesus to be that strong. They had misjudged who was actually in charge
the entire time.

## 6. What this is for

The seals are not God losing his temper. They are a mask coming off. Evil is allowed to run
its own course far enough that everyone can see where it leads.

And Jesus is not the only strong one in this story. He has an army — which is next time, and
it looks nothing like you would expect.

**Memory verse:** "The Lamb is worthy to open the scroll." (from Revelation 5:9)

## 7. Prayer

Jesus, thank you that you tell the truth about what is really evil, even when it looks
exciting. Thank you that when we cry "how long," you don't tell us off. Help us to wait
without giving up. Amen.

## Appendix: Ideas for leaders

**Opening activity:** The spot-the-fake game. Two similar objects, one real.

**Craft idea:** Four small cards showing the four horsemen in order, with one word each:
*looks great / fights / hunger / death.* Laying them in sequence makes the argument visually.

**Questions:**
1. Why do you think the first horseman looks so impressive?
2. Have you ever wanted something that turned out to be nothing like the advert?
3. What do you do when something is unfair and it doesn't get fixed straight away?

**Song suggestions:** A song about God being in control; a quieter song about waiting.

**Prep note:** Younger children in this range can find chapter 6 frightening. Keep the pace
brisk and land firmly on section 6 — the seals are a mask coming off, not God attacking.
