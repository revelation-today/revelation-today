# The Thousand Years
### Revelation 20

> **Story to read:** [The Millennium]({{< relref "story/elementary/content/1000y" >}})  
> **Bible:** {{% bible val="Revelation 20" link="rev:20" lang="en" %}}


## 1. Grown-ups disagreeing

Tell the group honestly: this is the chapter Christians argue about most. Sensible, godly
people read it differently, and have for centuries.

That is worth modelling. You are allowed to say "people disagree, and here is what we think,
and here is why."

## 2. The story so far, in one go

- Satan lost when Jesus came the first time — properly lost.
- Here he is not just thrown out of heaven but **chained in the abyss**, so that he cannot
  do what he most wants to do: deceive the nations.
- That is why the church can get on with its work. That is why the gospel goes everywhere.

## 3. Ruling, but not the way you think

Then we are told the church reigns with Christ.

And our kind of ruling looks strange from outside. It sometimes looks exactly like losing.
The text mentions those who were beheaded for their testimony — and says they are the ones
*reigning*.

That is the picture, and it is the same one from chapter 11: even at our weakest, even in
death, we are treated as royalty. Not despite the weakness. In it.

## 4. The last gasp

At the end Satan is released, gathers everything he has left, and attacks.

And it fizzles out. Fire comes down and it is finished — quicker than any battle in the book.
Like a firework that goes up impressively and then simply stops.

He has been building to this for twenty chapters, and it takes one verse.

## 5. The books

Then the great white throne, and books are opened, and everyone stands there — everyone, with
no exceptions and nowhere to be important.

And what matters is one thing: whether a person let Jesus in, and let that change how they
lived.

## 6. Saying this gently

Children of this age will ask about people they love. Be honest and be kind: we are told that
the Judge is the same Jesus who wept, who healed people nobody else would touch, and who died
for the very people who killed him.

We do not know everything about who is written in that book. We know exactly what the one
holding it is like.

**Memory verse:** "Blessed and holy are those who share in the first resurrection."
(Revelation 20:6)

## 7. Prayer

Jesus, thank you that Satan is chained and that his ending is already written. Thank you that
even when we are weak we are treated like kings. And thank you that the one who judges is the
one who loves us most. Amen.

## Appendix: Ideas for leaders

**Opening activity:** A firework picture, or a party popper if you can. Big noise, over
instantly. That is chapter 20's ending for Satan.

**Craft idea:** A chain of paper links the children write victories on — small ones from their
own week. Chain it around the room.

**Questions:**
1. Why do you think the people who were killed are described as reigning?
2. What does it tell us that Satan's last attack takes one verse?
3. What is the Judge like?

**Song suggestions:** A song about Jesus reigning; something reassuring to close.

**Prep note:** Say plainly at the start that Christians disagree here. Children who later meet
a different view will trust you more for having told them, and it teaches them that
disagreement among believers is normal rather than alarming.
