Revelation is the most quoted-out-of-context book in the Bible, and most of the confusion
around it doesn't come from the book being obscure. It comes from people opening it cold.

These **11 sessions** are the run-up. They're written for the same age group as the
[Teens track]({{< relref "kids/teens" >}}) — ages 13 to 18 — and they cover everything
Revelation assumes you already know and never stops to explain: the Roman world John's first
readers actually lived in, how apocalyptic writing works and why it isn't a coded timeline,
the promise God keeps making and Israel keeps breaking, and the Daniel visions that Revelation
borrows from on nearly every page.

| Sessions | What they cover |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | The Roman world, how to read this kind of book, and why its structure isn't random |
| 4–5 | The promise God keeps, and why you're included in Israel's story |
| 6–8 | Daniel: staying yourself under pressure, the countdown, and the Son of Man |
| 9–11 | Why God wants closeness rather than control, the Exodus pattern, and Babel and Balaam |

**You don't have to run this track first.** The Teens track stands on its own, and a group
that's short on time can go straight into Revelation. But the payoff is much larger if you
don't. Almost every image that feels bizarre on a first read — the beast, the horns, the
seventy weeks, the son of man on the clouds — is already sitting in these eleven sessions,
introduced where it originally came from, so that by the time it turns up in Revelation it
reads as a callback instead of a puzzle.

Each session works the same way as the Teens track: it grounds its teaching in the text
itself, names a place where you're already strong, names a real struggle worth growing in,
and opens space for the questions actually worth talking about.

When you finish, session 11 hands straight over to
[the first Revelation session]({{< relref "kids/teens/01-a-vision-too-big-to-ignore" >}}).
