{{% bible val="The magic number of the beast" link="rev:13,18" lang="en" %}} has been used, wrongly in my opinion, to decode current politics through one numbering scheme or another. The real answer isn't found by hunting outside the text — it's already there in the Bible itself.

The dragon and the two beasts in the book of Revelation are usually interpreted in one of two ways: either as purely abstract evil forces, or through very "creative" speculation about current events. Who are they really?

## The context of the first readers

<a name="2f6a"></a>
The key to understanding the dragon and the two beasts is to remember that this {{% int_link val="book is a letter" link="/expl/background/literature/the-book-of-revelation-how-to-read-it" %}} written to real first-century readers — a fact that gives us solid clues to the meaning of the {{% bible val="two beasts in Revelation" link="rev:13" lang="en" %}}.

The two beasts coming up out of the sea and out of the earth are based on:

- {{% bible val="Behemoth" link="job:40,15-24" lang="en" %}}, the beast from the earth, which only God, its maker, could approach with a sword ({{% bible val="Job 40:19" link="job:40,19" lang="en" %}}), and in Revelation, too, {{% bible val="a beast is wounded by a sword" link="rev:13,14" lang="en" %}}, and
- {{% bible val="Leviathan " link="job:41" lang="en" %}}, the beast from the sea, which has {{% bible val="flames coming out of its mouth" link="job:41,19" lang="en" %}}.

These beasts {{% bible val="are subdued by God" link="job:7,12" lang="en" %}}, and the Old Testament — including {{% bible val="Isaiah" link="isa:27,1" lang="en" %}} — expected God to conquer Leviathan fully in the end times; later Jewish tradition (2 Baruch 29, 4 Ezra 6) extends the same fate to Behemoth.

Human beings are no match for either beast, and on one reading of the Hebrew, {{% bible val="even a toy for God" link="psa:104,26" lang="en" %}} — though most modern translations render this as Leviathan playing *in* the sea rather than being God's plaything; either way, the beasts are firmly under God's power.

To the first readers, whatever came "out of the sea" meant something foreign. This echoes Daniel 7, where the four beasts likewise rise out of a chaotic sea — but it also had a concrete local resonance: the emperor in Rome, or the governors who all arrived by sea via Ephesus, where ships approaching over the horizon genuinely look like they're rising up out of the water.

"From the earth," by contrast, pointed to native political and economic authorities.

## The dragon

<a name="bb06"></a>
He's the easiest of the three to identify: he is the devil, the puppet master behind everything and the true origin of evil, since {{% bible val="we are not fighting against flesh and blood" link="eph:6,12" lang="en" %}}. He is not really the one holding power on earth — he drives the powers that are.

## The first beast

<a name="1c08"></a>
This is the emperor in Rome, coming from the sea. He is no different from all the rulers before him, and when a particular emperor dies — Nero, Tiberius, whoever — the first beast doesn't die with him. {{% bible val="With each new emperor, it comes back to life. Rome is unbeatable." link="rev:13,3-4" lang="en" %}}

{{% bible val="Rome blasphemes God" link="rev:13,4-6" lang="en" %}} by {{% int_link val="claiming to be like him" link="/expl/background/history/pax-romana-key-to-understand-the-book-of-revelation" %}}. The emperor commands the Roman legions and breaks anyone who won't play along: {{% bible val="he starts persecutions against God's people and is successful with it" link="rev:13,7-8" lang="en" %}}. All who are not written in the book of life bow their knees before him, for {{% bible val="he has the power and the saints can only endure" link="rev:13,10" lang="en" %}}.

## The second beast

<a name="2f0d"></a>
This is the wide network of people who benefit from the emperor's favor — through tax breaks, financial support, or other privileges (historically, the provincial high priests of the imperial cult and the leading families of the cities of Roman Asia). For them, the {{% int_link val="emperor is a guarantee of the gods' blessings" link="/expl/background/history/pax-romana-key-to-understand-the-book-of-revelation" %}}.

They make {{% bible val="sure that the whole earth worships the first beast" link="rev:13,12" lang="en" %}}. They perform processions, build temples, work in and for the temples, sell meat dedicated to the idols, attend feasts for the idols, and much more — a comprehensive, everyday activity that doesn't look evil at all, described starting from {{% bible val="the moment this second beast rises up, looking harmless, like a lamb" link="rev:13,11" lang="en" %}}.

The emperor's followers present him as {{% bible val="worthy of great signs and wonders" link="rev:13,13-14" lang="en" %}}.

But how can the statue made by the second beast actually speak? Ancient sources describe statues sometimes rigged by priests or officials with a stake in the outcome, using hidden mechanisms to give the appearance of a voice from the idol itself. A related motif appears already in {{% bible val="the book of Daniel" link="dan:3,4-12" lang="en" %}}, where a statue must be worshiped on pain of death, even though there it does not itself speak. The same dynamic of manufactured authority seems to be at work here — the {{% bible val="idol gets its power from the people who support it" link="rev:13,15" lang="en" %}}.

The people then receive a mark on their hands and foreheads. In the Old Testament, this was a {{% bible val="picture of the remembrance of the power of God" link="exo:13,16" lang="en" %}}, meant to secure loyalty: don't forget what you've seen here. For God, that meant the plagues in Egypt and the Exodus; for the devil, it's the system you cannot defeat.

If you refuse to fall in line, you {{% bible val="will be financially ruined " link="rev:13,16-17" lang="en" %}}.

## Some basics

<a name="0630"></a>
First, we need some background before looking at this in more detail:

- **Gematria**: This is the most common way people try to decode the number 666. [Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria) assigns each letter of the alphabet a numerical value, and if a name's letters add up to 666, that person is declared "the evil one." If a name doesn't fit, proponents switch to variants of the name, try a different language (Hebrew, Latin, Greek), or fall back on reading it as a triple six — which is how Ronald Wilson Reagan ended up accused of being the beast, since each of his three names has six letters. With enough patience, you [can map many names across history and the present to 666](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_of_the_beast), and that flexibility is exactly the problem: this method has been badly abused, because a lock that fits every key isn't worth much. That said, one gematria solution stands apart from the rest, because it isn't just numerically neat but historically anchored: transliterated into Hebrew, "Nero Caesar" (נרון קסר) sums letter-by-letter to exactly 666 (נ50 + ר200 + ו6 + נ50 + ק100 + ס60 + ר200). Spelled without the final Hebrew *nun* — which is how the Latin form would naturally transliterate — the same name sums to 616, which is precisely the variant reading attested in a handful of early manuscripts (including papyrus P115) and already known to Irenaeus in the second century, who mentions it even while preferring 666. That convergence of an independent textual variant with an independent gematria calculation is why most modern critical scholars regard Nero Caesar as the best-supported gematria solution, rather than one guess among equally plausible others.
- **Numbers**: In the Bible, numbers are often tied to specific events. {{% bible val="Moses fasted for 40 days when he received the Law at Mount Sinai" link="exo:34,28" lang="en" %}}, {{% bible val="the people spent 40 days scouting out the new land" link="num:13,25" lang="en" %}}, and {{% bible val="because of their unbelief they had to wander in the desert for 40 years" link="num:14,34" lang="en" %}}. Is it a coincidence that {{% bible val="Jesus fasted for 40 days in the desert, facing the same challenges" link="mat:4,1-11" lang="en" %}} Israel faced across their 40 years of wandering? You could say the number 40 is tied to {{% int_link val="acts of faith" link="/expl/background/structure/the-use-of-numbers-in-the-book-of-revelation" %}}. In the same way, a longstanding numerological tradition ties the number 7 to completion and to God, {{% bible val="who completed the world in 7 days" link="gen:2,1-3" lang="en" %}}, while the number 6 is traditionally read as pointing to man and to humanity's imperfect actions, since man was created on the sixth day. This symbolism isn't spelled out explicitly in Genesis itself, but it's an old and widespread interpretive convention. {{% int_link val="See more about numbers." link="/expl/background/structure/the-use-of-numbers-in-the-book-of-revelation" %}}
- **A letter**: We've already seen that {{% int_link val="we should always look out for clues that are relevant to the original readers" link="/expl/background/literature/the-book-of-revelation-how-to-read-it" %}}. So what would 666 have meant at the time of the early church?

## 6–6–6: The failure of the evil trinity

<a name="69b0"></a>
The first way of understanding it comes straight from the story in {{% bible val="Rev. 13" link="rev:13" lang="en" %}} itself, explained in more depth in {{% int_link val="another article" link="/expl/content/beasts/the-nature-of-the-beast-in-the-book-of-revelation" %}}: the beast's whole project is to imitate the Holy Trinity of God, Jesus, and the Holy Spirit.

Here's one illustrative way to picture the failure of that imitation — a memory device rather than a formula stated in the text itself — grading how well the counterfeit actually holds up on the points where it matters most:

- The dragon — is he really almighty, like the Father? No: he has been {{% bible val="thrown down from heaven" link="rev:12,9" lang="en" %}}, and the power he hands to the beast lasts {{% bible val="only 42 months" link="rev:13,5" lang="en" %}}. Score: 6.
- The first beast — did he really lay down his life out of love, and did he really die? No: even his death was a fake, since only one head was struck down. Score: 6.
- The second beast — does he really invite and counsel people the way the Holy Spirit does? No: he operates purely through fear and intimidation. Score: 6.

Add it up, and you get 6-6-6 — a grade of failure, three times over, on the very points where the imitation matters most. If you know the real God, the counterfeit can't fool you. Stay in the love, and you're safe.

## The 666 in context

<a name="2def"></a>
The number 666 also has some very interesting {{% int_link val="arithmetic properties" link="/expl/background/structure/the-use-of-numbers-in-the-book-of-revelation#6395" %}} and links elsewhere inside the book of Revelation.

- 666 connects to the number 8 by its arithmetic: 666 is the triangle of 36 (1 + 2 + 3 … + 36), and 36 is itself the triangle of 8 (1 + 2 … + 8). Eight is the number early Christian tradition associated with resurrection: Jesus rose "on the first day of the week" — the day after the Sabbath, so the first day of the new week, which the early church came to call the "eighth day," the first day of the new creation. Resurrection is also prominent in the {{% bible val="description of the first beast" link="rev:13,3" lang="en" %}}, in {{% bible val="the sixth bowl " link="rev:16,13" lang="en" %}} (whose frogs recall the Egyptian goddess Heqet, associated with birth and resurrection[^frogs]), and in the {{% bible val="description of the beast on which the harlot sits" link="rev:17,8" lang="en" %}}. There's a strong similarity, too, between [this passage's call for wisdom](https://biblehub.com/interlinear/revelation/13-18.htm) and the [call for wisdom about the beast the harlot sits on](https://biblehub.com/interlinear/revelation/17-9.htm), which is later {{% bible val="described as one of the seven heads (the kings) but also as an eighth king" link="rev:17,11" lang="en" %}}.
- The text calls this a {{% bible val="number of a man" link="rev:13,18" lang="en" %}} and places it directly before the {{% bible val="mention of the 144,000" link="rev:14,1" lang="en" %}}, which itself points forward to the New Jerusalem, [whose wall is measured at 12×12=144 cubits, by human measurement, which is also an angel's measurement](https://biblehub.com/interlinear/revelation/21-17.htm). Even though the exact meaning stays elusive, it underlines the same contrast again: the beast represents humanity debased, while the 144,000 are humanity redeemed ({{% bible val="14:3–4" link="rev:14,3-4" lang="en" %}}).[^measure]

Together, these point to two things:

- This mirrors the pattern at {{% int_link val="Armageddon" link="/expl/content/bowls/the-key-to-armageddon" %}}, where what looks impressive is exposed as utterly weak, while what looks weak turns out to be superior.
- The passage sets up the resolution of the mystery of 666 as the eighth king, taken up next.

## The eighth king

<a name="22e8"></a>
So who would this eighth king be? A side note, not a count: the seven kings stand for the whole line of rulers, and this site does not number them. But the returning eighth had a face the first readers knew. The story of Nero fits remarkably well. He was notorious for killing his own mother, yet he was also very popular with the common people of Rome. He alienated the Roman aristocracy but was genuinely popular, even adored, in the East — especially in Greece and among the Parthians. Even though the first Jewish war began in Nero's time, Jewish memory of him was more ambivalent than of Vespasian and Titus, who actually prosecuted the war on his behalf. For Christians, though, Nero was the one who launched the first persecutions, and many came to see him as the Antichrist.

When Nero realized he was about to die, rumors spread that he was fleeing east to his allies there. That never happened — he was surrounded, the Senate declared him a public enemy, and he killed himself with a dagger instead. Because he was denied a public state funeral, and few people had actually seen the body, a myth spread that he hadn't died at all. In the years that followed, a string of impostors claimed to be Nero, and several of them found major support from the Parthians.

This kept the myth of a returning Nero alive. Since he was now associated with the Parthians — a genuine threat to Rome — he increasingly came to be seen as judgment bearing down on Rome itself.

His unclear end, whether he was really dead or not, makes the {{% bible val="first beast's appearance a perfect resemblance of him" link="rev:13,3" lang="en" %}}. The {{% bible val="sixth bowl" link="rev:16,12" lang="en" %}} may point the same direction, since it could well be describing an attack by the Parthians.

## An illustration: Emperor Trajan

<a name="576c"></a>
It's also worth pointing to one later figure who illustrates the same pattern in his own generation, for two reasons:

- he was the emperor within the lifetime of the letter's early readers (though he only came to power in AD 98, a couple of years after Revelation is usually dated, under Domitian, around AD 95–96), and
- he {{% int_link val="reflects the dual attack of the beast" link="/expl/content/beasts/the-nature-of-the-beast-in-the-book-of-revelation" %}}.

The emperor who shows this most clearly is Trajan, who:

- reigned from 98–117, well within a generation of the letter's writing,
- ruled that Christians were not to be hunted down, but were to be punished if they were accused and refused to sacrifice,[^pliny] and yet
- was considered one of the [five good emperors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerva%E2%80%93Antonine_dynasty#Five_Good_Emperors): under his reign the Roman Empire reached its greatest extent, he established a substantial [welfare program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alimenta), carried out major [building projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan#Building_projects), and invested heavily in [public games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan#Games) — all of it embodying the [Pax Romana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Romana).

## Daniel's friends in the furnace

<a name="9bcc"></a>
A second way to understand the number comes from the story of {{% bible val="Daniel's friends in the fiery furnace" link="dan:3" lang="en" %}}, which has many parallels to the second beast. Here too there's a statue that people are required to worship, and whoever is caught disobeying is killed by fire. What's particularly interesting are the {{% bible val="dimensions of the statue" link="dan:3,1" lang="en" %}}: 60 cubits high (roughly 90 feet) and 6 cubits wide (roughly 9 feet) — the text gives only these two figures, so a square 6×6 base is a traditional inference rather than something stated outright.

Even more interesting is how the friends respond when confronted for {{% bible val="not bowing to the statue" link="dan:3,16-18" lang="en" %}}: "We trust that God can save us — but even if not, we will be faithful." This is where 666 touches our own response to oppression: those who don't comply are killed, or at least cannot buy or sell, facing real financial struggle. The call here is to stay faithful even if God doesn't visibly intervene.

But what does this have to do with the beast? Look at how the story in Daniel ends. Because the friends stayed faithful, they were preserved — "someone like the Son of God" stood with them in the fire — and the story closes with a happy ending. In trouble, we can be just as sure that Jesus is with us, and the next chapter of Revelation turns the tables the same way: the devil's followers end up in serious trouble, while the believers come through in good shape.

## Solomon's riches

<a name="719b"></a>
A third way: 666 appears written out exactly like that in the Bible, in {{% bible val="the summary of Solomon's wealth" link="1ki:10,14-29" lang="en" %}}, right at its opening — the last high point before {{% bible val="the description of his catastrophic failure" link="1ki:11,1-13" lang="en" %}}.

What is the story of Solomon? It begins with the {{% bible val="story of Bathsheba" link="2sa:11" lang="en" %}}, which led to {{% bible val="Nathan rebuking David, and to the loss of the child born from that sin" link="2sa:12,1-23" lang="en" %}} — yet {{% bible val="God gave him another child with Bathsheba, a sign of God's own love" link="2sa:12,24-25" lang="en" %}}. After Solomon was enthroned, {{% bible val="he asked God for an understanding heart instead of wealth and power — and because he asked for that, God gave him all the power and wealth" link="1ki:3" lang="en" %}} on top.

As a result, he came to see God as the source of his blessing, but the blessing itself gradually became more important to him than his relationship with God, and in the end, this {{% bible val="was his downfall" link="1ki:11,1-13" lang="en" %}}. And doesn't it seem strange that {{% bible val="he received exactly 666 talents of gold every year" link="1ki:10,14" lang="en" %}}, when every other number in that chapter is rounded?

## What to do?

<a name="0a91"></a>
So what can Christians do to confront this system? The answer is given, for example, in chapter 14: they stay faithful to God and refuse to compromise, described there {{% bible val="because they are virgins" link="rev:14,4" lang="en" %}}. In chapter 13, we see the mirror image: those who fail that same test end up {{% bible val="worshiping the beast instead" link="rev:13,8" lang="en" %}}.

And running through all of it: {{% bible val="perseverance" link="rev:13,10" lang="en" %}} and {{% bible val="wisdom to tell what is of God and what is not" link="rev:13,18" lang="en" %}}.

## Conclusion

<a name="89a3"></a>
So what is the takeaway, both for the first listeners and for us?

- 666 shows that the great, overpowering, controlling beast is ultimately a failure, so believers need not be afraid.
- We need to stay strong and not be overcome by fear when things get hard — persecution, the fear of death, economic exclusion — even when God doesn't visibly step in to intervene; that's the lesson of Daniel's friends.
- We shouldn't be tempted by an attractive compromise, telling ourselves we're still following God while really trying to have the best of both God and the world, the way Solomon's compromise eventually failed him.
- Trajan illustrates the same danger in his own time: he seemed to bring wealth and security, yet had the blood of Christians on his hands.

So today, it's worth examining where we see the same {{% int_link val="principles of the 666" link="/expl/content/beasts/the-nature-of-the-beast-in-the-book-of-revelation" %}} at work around us, and judging whether they:

- follow God's principles and will therefore remain, or
- follow the principles of the devil, which may look tempting now but will end in disaster and pull us away from God.

If you think 666 refers to some computer chip waiting to be implanted, ask yourself this instead: what information could such a chip provide that isn't already available through

- Your Google search
- Browsing history
- Smartphone usage (including location tracking)
- Credit or debit card usage
- Tons of social media records?
