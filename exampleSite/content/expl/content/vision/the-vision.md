The book of Revelation begins with a bang: John has a vision of an amazing figure walking among the lampstands with stars in hand. It's clear that this vision somehow sets the scope for the whole book — but what exactly is the message?

## Overview

<a name="3876"></a>
John — most likely the apostle (the traditional identification, though a minority of scholars have argued for a separate figure known as "John the Elder") — is introduced as someone {{% bible val="on the island of Patmos because of the word of God and the testimony of Jesus" link="rev:1,9" lang="en" %}}. Tradition says he was exiled there; whether that was a formal Roman banishment or a mission field he chose is debated. The vision he receives of Jesus falls into three parts:

- the vision itself (verses 12–16),
- his reaction (verse 17a),
- and the interpretation (verses 17b through 20).

## The vision

<a name="7487"></a>
Several elements of the vision are worth pausing on:

- **The lampstands.** The first thing John sees is seven lampstands, most likely drawn {{% bible val="from Zechariah" link="zec:4,2-10" lang="en" %}} — if you're not familiar with that book, [here is a brief summary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_106IfO6Kc0). In that chapter, the seven lamps represent the Holy Spirit. Their identity here stays unclear until the interpretation later in the passage.
- **The Son of Man.** In the midst of the lampstands stands one like the Son of Man — a well-known image from {{% bible val="Daniel" link="dan:7,13" lang="en" %}} that [Jesus applied to himself](https://www.bibleserver.com/search/NIV/son%20of%20man). Since {{% bible val="Jesus also holds the role of a priest" link="heb:7,11-28" lang="en" %}}, it is his {{% bible val="responsibility to tend the lampstands" link="exo:30,7" lang="en" %}}, just as it was the priest's job in the temple.
- **Robe with a golden sash.** The robe and sash Jesus wears could be read as a priestly garment or a royal one — an ambiguity that may well be intentional, since Jesus is presented as both priest and king.
- **White hair.** In Daniel 7 the white hair does not belong to the Son of Man. It belongs to the {{% bible val="Ancient of Days" link="dan:7,9" lang="en" %}} — to God himself.
- **Eyes like blazing fire, legs like burnished bronze, a loud voice.** These are all {{% bible val="characteristics of Daniel 10" link="dan:10,6" lang="en" %}}, where a {{% bible val="figure identified as an angelic warrior" link="dan:10,20" lang="en" %}} prepares Daniel for his final vision — a role very much like the one this figure plays for John here.
- **A sword out of his mouth.** Not a weapon in his hand. His only weapon is his word, and it is used twice in the book: against the nations at the end ({{% bible val="19:15, 21" link="rev:19,15" lang="en" %}}) and, in the letter to Pergamon, against the church itself ({{% bible val="2:16" link="rev:2,16" lang="en" %}}).
- **A face like the sun.** The brightness of God's own presence, as on the mountain where {{% bible val="Jesus was transfigured" link="mat:17,2" lang="en" %}}. At the end of the book the city needs no sun, because {{% bible val="God and the Lamb are its light" link="rev:21,23" lang="en" %}}.

So the Son of Man is given the appearance of God: the two figures of Daniel 7 are laid over each other in one person. The features from Daniel 10 do something else — they give him the bearing of the one who prepares a prophet for what he is about to see.

## The response

<a name="8d12"></a>
John collapses, as {{% bible val="Daniel did at his visions" link="dan:10,8-19" lang="en" %}}.

## The interpretation

<a name="b723"></a>
Then Jesus says who he is: {{% bible val="the First and the Last, the Living One, who was dead and is alive for ever, and who holds the keys of death and Hades" link="rev:1,17-18" lang="en" %}}. That is the answer to John's collapse, and it says in the vision's own words what {{% bible val="the opening of the letter" link="rev:1,5" lang="en" %}} had already claimed — the faithful witness, the firstborn from the dead, the ruler of the kings of the earth.

Finally, the {{% bible val="mystery of the lampstands and stars is solved" link="rev:1,20" lang="en" %}}: they represent {{% int_link val="different descriptions of the church" link="/expl/content/letters/the-angel-of-the-churches" %}}. So what does that mean?

The church is described through two different images, which share two things in common:

- Both are sources of light: the stars provide orientation in creation, and the lampstand is the only source of light in the temple.
- Both depend on Jesus: the stars cannot shine unless they are in his hand, and the lamp only stays lit if the oil — the Holy Spirit — is there.

The point of the church is to be light for the world, so that the world can be saved. That is the point of the book, and it is what the church is tested on: is the church a witness convincing enough to reach the nations?
